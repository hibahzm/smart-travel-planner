## Peru — Destination Guide

Peru is the archaeological heart of South America, home to Machu Picchu, the Amazon basin, the Nazca Lines, and the floating reed islands of Lake Titicaca. It is also one of the world's great food destinations — Lima has more restaurants in San Pellegrino's World's Top 50 than any other Latin American city. Costs are low by global standards; the experiences are in the top tier globally.

## Overview

Peru divides into three zones: the Costa (arid Pacific coast, Lima, Nazca), the Sierra (Andes highlands, Cusco, Machu Picchu, Lake Titicaca), and the Selva (Amazon basin, Iquitos, Puerto Maldonado). Most visitors combine Lima with the Cusco–Machu Picchu circuit and optionally Lake Titicaca.

## Best Time to Visit

**Dry season (May–October):** The highland dry season is ideal for Machu Picchu and the Inca Trail. June and July are peak months. The Inti Raymi festival (Festival of the Sun) in Cusco on June 24 is spectacular but books out completely — reserve accommodation 6 months ahead. The south is cold at night (0–5°C at altitude) but clear and sunny.

**Wet season (November–April):** The Amazon is best visited November–May (higher water levels, more wildlife visible from boats). The highlands receive heavy rain; the Inca Trail closes entirely in February for maintenance. Machu Picchu is more atmospheric in the mist (and less crowded) November–March.

## Getting In

Lima Jorge Chávez International Airport (LIM) is the hub. Direct flights from Miami (6 hr), New York (8 hr), Madrid (12 hr). LATAM, American, Delta, Iberia. Domestic connections: Lima–Cusco (1.5 hr, $60–150 on LATAM or Sky Peru) are frequently the bottleneck — book months ahead for May–September.

## Getting Around

**Lima–Cusco:** Fly; the bus is 20+ hours. Buses (Cruz del Sur, CIVA) are suitable for Lima–Nazca (6 hr, $20), Cusco–Puno (7 hr, $15–25), and Puno–Copacabana (Bolivia, 4 hr, $10).

**Cusco–Machu Picchu:** By train from Ollantaytambo (90 min, $60–80 one way, PeruRail or Inca Rail) + bus from Aguas Calientes to the ruins (30 min, $12 return). The Inca Trail is the alternative (4 days, $600–900 including permit and guide) — permits must be booked 6–12 months in advance and sell out completely.

## Machu Picchu

The Inca citadel sits at 2,430m on a ridge above the Urubamba River. It is undeniably one of the world's great sights; arriving at sunrise through the Sun Gate (Inti Punku, a 2-hour add-on hike) before the tour buses makes it transcendent. Entry: $45–60 (two time slots: 6–12pm, 12–5:30pm). Maximum 4,500 visitors per day; book at machupicchutickets.machupicchu.gob.pe at least 2 weeks ahead in peak season.

**Huayna Picchu (the iconic peak above the ruins):** Limited to 400 visitors/day. Book simultaneously with main entry — sells out months ahead.

**Altitude:** Cusco sits at 3,400m. Allow 2 days acclimatisation before strenuous activity. Symptoms of altitude sickness (headache, nausea) are common; coca tea helps; rest and hydration are mandatory. Acetazolamide (Diamox) prevents symptoms — consult a doctor before travel.

## Cusco

The former Inca capital is a UNESCO World Heritage city where Inca stonework forms the foundations of Spanish colonial buildings. The Plaza de Armas is one of South America's greatest city squares.

- **Boleto Turístico ($45):** Covers 16 sites including Sacsayhuamán fortress, Qorikancha temple, Moray terraces. Valid 10 days.
- **San Pedro Market:** Local market, not touristy. Breakfast: fresh juices (S/. 3), empanadas, quinoa porridge.
- **Pisac Market (Sunday):** The Sacred Valley's largest craft market. Authentic textiles; bargain to 40–50% of opening price.

## Amazon (Puerto Maldonado)

Southern Amazon gateway. Tambopata National Reserve offers accessible wildlife viewing: giant river otters, macaw clay licks, caimans. Lodge + guide packages: $150–400/day. Flights from Lima: ~$100.

## Lima Food Scene

Lima's Miraflores and Barranco neighbourhoods concentrate world-class restaurants. Central (chef Virgilio Martínez, currently ranked #1 in the world) requires booking 3+ months ahead, costs $200/person. Accessible alternatives:

- Maido (Nikkei cuisine, top 10 globally): $80–100/person
- La Mar (cevichería): $20–35/person
- Street ceviche at Mercado Surquillo: $8–12 — arguably better than anywhere formal

## Costs

- Budget: $35–50/day (hostels, local restaurants, buses)
- Mid-range: $70–120/day
- Luxury (Lima 5-star + Machu Picchu Belmond Sanctuary Lodge): $400–800/day

## Practical Tips

- Machu Picchu tickets: book online only at the official government portal. Third-party agencies charge 30–50% premiums.
- Inca Trail: only 500 permits per day (including guides and porters). Your operator handles the booking; you provide passport details. Close to impossible to book less than 3 months ahead for June–August.
- Coca leaves: legal in Peru; widely chewed or made into tea for altitude acclimatisation. Illegal to bring home to most countries.
- Safety: Cusco and Machu Picchu are safe for tourists. Lima's Miraflores and Barranco are safe; avoid Centre Lima at night.
