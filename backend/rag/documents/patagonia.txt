## Patagonia — Destination Guide (Chile & Argentina)

Patagonia is the southern cone shared between Chile and Argentina, encompassing some of the most dramatic and least-visited wilderness on earth. The Torres del Paine massif in Chilean Patagonia, Los Glaciares National Park in Argentine Patagonia (home to Perito Moreno glacier), and the wild Carretera Austral highway are the principal draws for serious adventure travellers.

## Overview

Patagonia spans approximately 1 million km² of steppe, ice fields, fjords, and granite spires. The weather is notoriously extreme and unpredictable — four seasons in a day is not hyperbole. Wind speeds of 100+ km/h on the altiplano are common. The infrastructure is sparse by design; this is one of the world's last true wilderness destinations.

Principal destinations:
- **Torres del Paine National Park (Chile):** The W Trek and O Circuit trek, the iconic granite towers.
- **El Calafate (Argentina):** Gateway to Perito Moreno glacier and Los Glaciares NP.
- **El Chaltén (Argentina):** The trekking capital. Base for Fitz Roy and Cerro Torre.
- **Carretera Austral (Chile):** 1,240 km of mostly unpaved highway through remote fjord landscape.
- **Ushuaia (Argentina):** Southernmost city, gateway to Tierra del Fuego and Antarctica cruises.

## Best Time to Visit

**November–March (Southern Hemisphere summer):** The only practical window. Peak season is December–February — book campsites and refugios in Torres del Paine 6–12 months in advance. November and March offer better prices and fewer crowds with similar weather. April is shoulder season; snow arrives in May.

## Getting In

**Punta Arenas (Chile):** Main gateway for Torres del Paine. LATAM and Sky Airline fly from Santiago (3.5 hr, $100–200 round trip). Bus from Punta Arenas to Puerto Natales (the park gateway): 3 hours, ~$12. Bus from Puerto Natales to the park entrance: 2 hours, ~$15.

**El Calafate (Argentina):** LATAM and Aerolíneas Argentinas fly from Buenos Aires (3 hr, $150–300). Taxi to town: 20 minutes. From here, Perito Moreno glacier is a 1.5-hour bus ride ($10).

## Torres del Paine — Trekking

**The W Trek (4–5 days):** The classic route. Covers the three towers, the Valle del Francés, and the Grey Glacier. Doable in guesthouses (refugios: $40–80/night including dinner and breakfast) or camping (wild and designated sites).

**The O Circuit (8–11 days):** Adds the backside of the massif. Less crowded, more demanding. Requires proper gear and experience. Refugio space on the back circuit is limited; you will camp some nights.

**Booking:** CONAF (Chile's national park authority) controls access. All campsites and refugios must be booked online at the CONAF portal before arrival. You cannot show up and camp where you like. Peak season fills entirely within minutes of the booking window opening (usually in August for the following season).

**Park entry:** ~$35 USD (foreigners). Weather windows are short — you may wait 2–3 days in Puerto Natales for a wind break.

## Perito Moreno Glacier

The only major advancing glacier on Earth; it calves into Lago Argentino with thunderous explosions. Access via boardwalks directly above the glacier face — free to walk once inside Los Glaciares NP ($22 entry). Ice trekking on the glacier: $90–130 through local operators (Big Ice or Mini Trekking options). Boat excursions around the glacier face: ~$30.

## Costs

Patagonia is expensive relative to surrounding South America due to remoteness. Expect:
- Budget camping + self-catering: $65–80/day
- Mid-range refugio trekking: $120–180/day
- Luxury lodge (Explora, Awasi): $600–1,500/night

Food in Puerto Natales: restaurant meal $15–25. Self-catering from the supermarket in Puerto Natales before entering the park dramatically cuts costs.

## Essential Gear

This list is non-negotiable for safety:
- Windproof, waterproof jacket (Gore-Tex or equivalent)
- Trekking poles
- Gaiters
- Layers (temperatures swing 15°C in a day)
- Trekking boots broken in before arrival (blisters at the start of the W are miserable)
- Buff/balaclava for wind
- Sunscreen (UV is intense at southern latitudes, especially on glaciers)

## Practical Tips

- Peso (ARS in Argentina) situation: Argentina's parallel exchange rate (blue dollar) can be 2× the official rate. Bring USD cash in small bills for blue-rate exchanges in Buenos Aires before flying south.
- Fuel on the Carretera Austral: fill up whenever you see a pump. Towns are 150+ km apart.
- Cell service: essentially zero inside parks. Download offline maps (Maps.me or CalTopo).
- Permit your generator: the Patagonian wind is relentless. Don't underestimate tent peg requirements.
