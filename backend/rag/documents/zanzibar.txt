# Zanzibar, Tanzania

## Overview
Zanzibar is an archipelago off Tanzania's coast in the Indian Ocean, comprising Unguja (the main island, usually called Zanzibar), Pemba, and dozens of smaller islets. Its history as a spice island, slave trade hub, and Omani Sultanate capital has left an extraordinary cultural legacy: the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Stone Town, a Swahili-Arab-Indian-Portuguese architectural fusion, and a multicultural society that produced, among others, Freddie Mercury. Its beaches are among Africa's best.

## Getting There
Abeid Amani Karume International Airport (ZNZ) on Unguja receives direct flights from Nairobi (1h), Dar es Salaam (20 min), and seasonal charter flights from Europe (mainly UK and Germany). Most travellers combine Zanzibar with a Tanzania safari itinerary, flying directly from the Serengeti or Ngorongoro via Dar es Salaam. The high-speed ferry from Dar es Salaam to Stone Town takes 1.5–2 hours (USD 35–50); several operators run services daily.

## Getting Around
**Stone Town** is compact and best explored on foot; the labyrinthine alleys are part of its appeal.
**Daladalas** — shared minibuses; cheap (TZS 500–2,000) but slow and crowded; the main form of inter-island transport for budget travellers.
**Shared taxis (dala dala shuttles)** — slightly more comfortable; organised by tour operators.
**Private taxis** — easily arranged from hotels; rates should be agreed in advance. A taxi from Stone Town to Nungwi beach costs approximately USD 25–35.
**Rental scooters** — available in Nungwi and Paje; useful for day trips.
**Boat tours** — essential for dolphin watching, Prison Island, Mnemba Atoll, and sandbank picnics.

## Stone Town
The historic core of Zanzibar City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — a maze of coral rag buildings with distinctive carved wooden doors, ornate balconies, and narrow streets barely wide enough for two people.
- **House of Wonders (Beit-al-Ajaib)** — the largest and tallest building in Stone Town; built 1883 as the Sultan's ceremonial palace; currently being restored.
- **Old Fort (Ngome Kongwe)** — 17th-century Portuguese fort converted to an Omani fortress; now a cultural centre with evening taarab music performances.
- **Slave Market Memorial** — the Anglican Cathedral built on the site of East Africa's largest slave market; the underground slave chambers remain as a sobering memorial.
- **Freddie Mercury's birthplace** — the Queen frontman was born in Stone Town in 1946 (as Farrokh Bulsara); a plaque marks the house.
- **Darajani Market** — the central market; fresh fish, meat, fruits, and the spices that made Zanzibar wealthy.
- **Forodhani Gardens** — seafront park transforming at night into an open-air food market with grilled seafood, Zanzibar pizza, and sugarcane juice.

## Beaches
Zanzibar's beaches are world-class: white coral sand, turquoise water, and consistent Indian Ocean warmth (26–29°C year-round).

**Nungwi (north)** — the most popular beach; calm, lagoon-style swimming at high tide; good for non-divers; lively bar and restaurant scene; many water sports operators.

**Kendwa (north)** — immediately south of Nungwi; famous for its full moon parties; slightly more relaxed than Nungwi.

**Paje (east coast)** — kitesurfing capital of East Africa; consistent trade winds; colourful coral reef at low tide. Best: June–October (southeast monsoon) for kitesurfers; avoid during heavy rain season.

**Jambiani (east coast)** — quieter, more authentically Zanzibari village feel; seaweed farming women visible at low tide.

**Matemwe (northeast)** — upmarket boutique hotels; facing Mnemba Atoll; excellent for diving and snorkelling; relatively unspoilt.

**Bwejuu (southeast)** — low-key fishing village; long empty beaches; excellent for escaping other tourists.

## Mnemba Atoll
A private island 3 km off Matemwe beach, surrounded by one of the Indian Ocean's most pristine coral reefs. The surrounding waters are a marine conservation area:
- Sea turtle nesting site (green turtles); encounters while diving or snorkelling are common.
- Spinner dolphins; morning trips for swimming with wild dolphins.
- 40+ species of hard coral and hundreds of reef fish species.
- Diving and snorkelling day trips run from Matemwe and Chwaka.

## Spice Tours
Zanzibar's historical role as the world's leading clove producer (supplying over 90% of global demand at its peak) can be experienced on spice farm tours (USD 20–35, arranged from any hotel). The farms around Kidichi and Kizimbani grow cloves, nutmeg, cinnamon, vanilla, lemongrass, and black pepper — smelling, tasting, and wearing spice-made jewellery. Most tours include a lunch of local Swahili food.

## Eat and Drink
Zanzibari cuisine is a fusion of Swahili, Arab, Indian, and Portuguese influences:
- **Zanzibari pizza** — thin egg-and-flour crepe folded around meat, cheese, and vegetables; sold at Forodhani Gardens for USD 1.
- **Urojo (Zanzibar mix)** — the most complex street dish; a curried broth with fried potatoes, cassava chips, bhajia, and hard-boiled egg.
- **Grilled octopus** — served on the beach at Jambiani and Paje; simply spiced and charcoal-grilled.
- **Pilau rice** — fragrant spiced rice with meat or fish; the signature Swahili rice dish.
- **Tamarind juice and sugar cane juice** — refreshing street drinks.
- **Seafood** — lobster, prawns, kingfish, and octopus are all caught locally; expensive by local standards but cheap by international ones.
- Alcohol is available in tourist hotels and restaurants; Stone Town is an Islamic city and drinking in public is discouraged.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Tanzanian Shilling (TZS); USD widely accepted in tourist areas.
- **Language**: Swahili and Arabic; English spoken in tourist contexts.
- **Safety**: Generally safe; petty theft in Stone Town (especially after dark); swim only in designated areas (strong currents on east coast at low tide).
- **Best time to visit**: June–October (dry season, southeast monsoon for kitesurfers) and December–February (short dry season). March–May is the long rains (heavy and persistent).
- **Budget**: Mid-range USD 80–150/day; significant variation between budget guesthouses (USD 30–50) and luxury beach resorts (USD 300–800+).
- **Health**: Malaria is present; take prophylaxis. Yellow fever vaccination required if arriving from endemic countries.
