## Kyoto, Japan — Destination Guide

Kyoto served as Japan's imperial capital for over a millennium and remains its cultural and spiritual heart. With 17 UNESCO World Heritage sites, more Michelin-starred restaurants than any city outside Tokyo, and neighbourhoods that look almost identical to their 17th-century form, it rewards slow travel more than almost anywhere on earth.

## Overview

Kyoto sits in a basin surrounded on three sides by forested mountains. The city divides into distinct districts: Central Kyoto (Kawaramachi, Shijo) for shopping and nightlife; Eastern Kyoto (Higashiyama) for temple-hopping and traditional machiya townhouses; Northern Kyoto (Kinkakuji, Kitayama) for quieter shrines; Arashiyama in the west for bamboo forests and boat rides; and Fushimi in the south for the iconic torii-gate paths.

## Best Time to Visit

**Spring (late March–early May):** Cherry blossom season. Maruyama Park and the Philosopher's Path become extraordinary; accommodation books out a year in advance and prices triple. Peak week (last week March – first week April) is spectacular but overwhelming.

**Autumn (mid-November–late November):** Autumn foliage. The maple (momiji) colours at Eikan-do, Tofuku-ji, and Arashiyama are among the most photographed in Japan. Slightly less crowded than spring.

**Summer (July–August):** Hot and humid, 30–35°C. The Gion Matsuri festival (all of July) is one of Japan's greatest festivals — elaborate floats, traditional music, street food stalls on Shijo-dori. Worth the heat.

**Winter (December–February):** Fewest tourists. Snow on Kinkaku-ji's golden roof is genuinely beautiful. Many outdoor attractions are at their quietest. Average 5°C, drops to freezing at night.

## Getting In

**From Tokyo:** Shinkansen (Nozomi) from Tokyo Station to Kyoto Station: 2h15m, ¥14,170 (~$95 USD). Buy the JR Pass before entering Japan if you plan to use Shinkansen more than twice — it pays off at 7-day ($280) or 14-day ($450) increments.

**From Osaka:** Local train (Hankyu or JR) from Osaka Umeda to Kawaramachi: 40 minutes, ¥410 (~$2.75). Many travellers base themselves in Osaka and day-trip.

**From KIX (Kansai Airport):** Haruka express to Kyoto Station: 75 minutes, ¥3,270 (~$22).

## Getting Around

Kyoto's bus network covers most attractions. A one-day bus pass costs ¥700 ($4.70). Bicycle hire is excellent and recommended for Arashiyama–Sagano, the Philosopher's Path, and Fushimi: ¥1,000–1,500/day. The subway has two lines and is faster than buses for the east–west corridor.

## Costs

Kyoto is Japan's priciest city outside Tokyo. Budget: $80–100/day. Mid-range: $150–200. Luxury ryokan with kaiseki dinner: $400–900/night per person.

- Bowl of ramen: ¥900–1,200 ($6–8)
- Set lunch at a mid-range restaurant: ¥1,500–2,500 ($10–17)
- Kaiseki (traditional multi-course) dinner: ¥15,000–50,000 ($100–340)
- Temple entry: ¥500–1,000 ($3.40–6.70) per site; most require separate tickets
- JR pass (7-day): ¥50,000 ($340)

## Top Attractions

**Fushimi Inari Taisha:** The mountain of 10,000 vermillion torii gates is Kyoto's most photographed sight. Free to enter, open 24 hours. Go at 5:30am or after 8pm to have the paths near the base to yourself. The full circuit (4 hours to the summit) gets quiet above the halfway point even in peak season.

**Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion):** Iconic gold-leaf temple reflected in a mirror pond. ¥500 entry. Arrive at opening (9am) — the approach path queues badly after 10am.

**Arashiyama Bamboo Grove:** 5-minute walk from Tenryu-ji. The bamboo tunnel is genuinely ethereal at 6am with morning light. Most tourists arrive by tour bus around 10am. Hire a rickshaw (jinrikisha) for ¥4,000/30 min for the full Arashiyama circuit.

**Philosopher's Path (Tetsugaku no Michi):** A 2km canal path lined with cherry trees connecting Nanzen-ji to Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion). Perfect for a slow morning walk with stops at small coffee shops and galleries.

**Nishiki Market ("Kyoto's Kitchen"):** A 400-year-old covered arcade, 5 lanes wide, selling pickles, tofu skin, grilled skewers, and street food. Open 9am–6pm. Try the fresh yudofu (tofu hot pot) from Nishiki Warai.

**Gion:** Kyoto's geisha district. Most atmospheric at dusk on Hanamikoji Street. Photographing geiko (Kyoto geisha) on the street is controversial — be respectful, do not touch or block them.

## Practical Tips

- Book accommodation months in advance for cherry blossom and autumn foliage seasons — the city literally runs out of rooms.
- IC card (Suica/ICOCA): load ¥3,000–5,000 on arrival for buses, trains, and convenience stores.
- Shoes you can slip off easily: all traditional spaces require shoe removal.
- Quiet hours in Higashiyama: photography of residents is unwelcome in residential lanes. Several areas have banned street photography.
- Coin lockers at Kyoto Station: ¥500–700 per day; essential for luggage during day-trip days.
