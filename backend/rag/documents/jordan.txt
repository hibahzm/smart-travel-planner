# Jordan

## Overview
Jordan is the Middle East's most visitor-friendly destination — stable, welcoming, and packed with extraordinary ancient sites. Petra, one of the Seven Wonders of the World, alone justifies the journey, but Jordan offers much more: Wadi Rum's otherworldly desert, the Dead Sea's surreal buoyancy, the Roman city of Jerash, and Amman's vibrant food scene. Jordan is a safe and accessible window into Arab culture and history.

## Getting There
Queen Alia International Airport (AMM) in Amman is the main entry point, with direct flights from most European cities, Gulf hubs (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha), and some North American routes. Royal Jordanian and budget carriers (Wizz Air, easyJet seasonal) connect Amman to Europe. Aqaba (AQJ) receives seasonal charter flights from Europe and is useful for Red Sea diving itineraries. Land crossings from Israel (the Allenby/King Hussein Bridge) are common for travellers doing Israel-Jordan combined tours; land entry from Saudi Arabia is also possible.

## Getting There — Visas
Most nationalities can obtain a visa on arrival (JOD 40) or purchase the **Jordan Pass** (JOD 70–80) before travel, which includes a visa and entry to 40+ attractions including Petra. The Jordan Pass is excellent value for any itinerary longer than 2 days in Petra.

## Getting Around
Jordan is compact (89,000 km²) and most routes are manageable in a day's drive. A rental car from Amman gives maximum flexibility (USD 25–50/day). JETT buses run comfortable air-conditioned service between Amman, Petra, and Aqaba. Private taxis and minibuses serve inter-city routes. In Amman, Uber and local taxis are cheap and safe.

## Amman
Jordan's capital is an underrated city worth 1–2 days:
- **Citadel (Jabal al-Qal'a)** — ruins of temples, Byzantine churches, and an Umayyad palace on a hill above the city; free entry and excellent views.
- **Roman Theatre** — 6,000-seat 2nd-century theatre still occasionally hosting performances; below the Citadel.
- **Jordan Archaeological Museum** — housed on the Citadel; free; Neolithic art from Ain Ghazal (9,000 years old) is world-significant.
- **Rainbow Street and Jabal Amman** — the city's bohemian quarter; cafes, galleries, and the best people-watching in Amman.
- **Souk Jara** (Fridays, May–October) — outdoor artisan market on Rainbow Street.

## Petra
Petra is the reason most travellers come to Jordan. The ancient Nabataean capital (4th century BCE–3rd century CE) was carved directly into pink sandstone cliffs.
- **The Siq** — 1.2-km slot canyon approach to Petra; the dramatic revelation of the Treasury at the end is one of travel's great moments.
- **The Treasury (Al-Khazneh)** — the iconic facade; likely a royal tomb, not actually a treasury (the name comes from the Bedouin legend of treasures hidden in the urn atop).
- **The Colonnaded Street and Royal Tombs** — the main street of the ancient city; 800 carved façades line the surrounding cliffs.
- **The Monastery (Ad-Deir)** — larger than the Treasury; reached by 800 carved steps; the climb is worth every step. Sunset from the viewpoint is spectacular.
- **Little Petra (Siq al-Barid)** — smaller Nabataean site 8 km north; less visited and free with Jordan Pass.
- **Petra by Night** — Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday evenings; hundreds of candles illuminate the Siq and Treasury. Beautiful but commercialised; skip if you've already seen it by day.
- **Practical**: Allow 2 full days minimum for Petra. The standard tour covers the main sites; 2 days allows the Monastery, back trails, and Al-Wu'Aira Crusader Castle.

## Wadi Rum
Jordan's most spectacular desert landscape — towering sandstone and granite mountains, red sand dunes, and a silence that approaches the absolute.
- **Bedouin camps** — the primary accommodation; from basic tents to luxury "bubble" camps under transparent domes for stargazing.
- **4WD jeep tours** — the standard way to explore; full-day tours cover Lawrence's Spring, Khazali Canyon, red sand dunes, and ancient rock inscriptions.
- **Hiking and scrambling** — routes up Jebel Rum and Jebel Khazali for experienced walkers; guided climbs on the famous sandstone faces.
- **Camel rides** — short sunset rides are common; multi-day camel treks to Petra are possible for the adventurous.
- The area was used as a filming location for The Martian, Rogue One, and Dune.

## Dead Sea
At 430 m below sea level, the lowest point on Earth, the Dead Sea is 34% salinity — 10 times saltier than the ocean. The buoyancy is extraordinary (impossible to sink). Floating while reading a newspaper is the obligatory photo.
- **Beaches**: Public beaches are free but basic; resort beaches (Kempinski, Movenpick, Crown Plaza) charge USD 30–60 day-use fees but include loungers, pools, and towels.
- **Mineral mud**: The Dead Sea's black mineral-rich mud is famous for skin benefits; apply, dry in the sun, and rinse off.
- **Declining water level**: The Dead Sea is shrinking at an alarming rate (1 m/year); sinkholes are forming along the shoreline. Visit sooner rather than later.
- From Amman: 60 km, about 1 hour.

## Other Sites
- **Jerash** — 30 km north of Amman; one of the best-preserved Roman cities in the world; the colonnaded street, hippodrome, and oval plaza rival Pompeii.
- **Wadi Mujib** — the "Grand Canyon of Jordan"; the Siq Trail through the gorge involves waist-deep wading; beautiful and thrilling.
- **Aqaba** — Jordan's only sea access; Red Sea diving and snorkelling; beach resort town; warmer than most of Jordan.
- **Ajloun Castle** — 12th-century Arab castle in pine forests north of Amman.
- **Umm Qais** — Roman ruins at the northern tip; extraordinary views over the Sea of Galilee and the Golan Heights.

## Eat and Drink
Jordanian cuisine is Levantine in tradition — generous, fresh, and hospitable:
- **Mansaf** — the national dish; lamb slow-cooked in jameed (dried fermented goat cheese broth) and served over rice with nuts; eaten communally with the right hand.
- **Meze** — hummus, mutabbal (smoked aubergine dip), tabouleh, fattoush, and stuffed vine leaves; the foundation of every Jordanian meal.
- **Falafel** — freshly fried; breakfast staple served in flatbread with salads and pickles.
- **Knafeh** — sweet shredded wheat pastry with cheese, soaked in sugar syrup; Nablus-style from local bakeries.
- **Arabic coffee (qahwa)** — lightly roasted, cardamom-scented; always poured in small cups; symbolises hospitality.
- **Alcohol**: Beer (Amstel brewed locally), arak, and wine are available in restaurants and bars in Amman and Aqaba. Jordan is more liberal than neighbouring countries.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Jordanian Dinar (JOD); 1 JOD ≈ USD 1.41 (one of the world's strongest currencies).
- **Language**: Arabic; English widely spoken in tourism contexts.
- **Safety**: One of the Middle East's safest countries; Jordan has maintained stability while surrounded by regional conflicts.
- **Best time to visit**: March–May and September–November (15–25°C). Summers are hot (35–40°C) but sites are less crowded.
- **Budget**: Mid-range USD 80–140/day including accommodation, transport, and site entry (Jordan Pass saves significant money).
- **Hospitality**: Jordanians are extraordinarily welcoming; accepting tea or coffee from strangers is a cultural norm, not a prelude to a sale.
