# Sri Lanka

## Overview
Sri Lanka is a teardrop-shaped island in the Indian Ocean with a remarkable concentration of experiences within a small area (65,610 km²). Ancient cities, sacred Buddhist temples, lush tea highlands, elephant-filled national parks, world-class surf breaks, and tropical beaches are all accessible within a single two-week itinerary. Emerging from decades of civil conflict and recovering from the 2022 economic crisis, Sri Lanka is once again one of Asia's most rewarding travel destinations.

## Getting There
Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) in Colombo handles almost all international arrivals. Direct flights connect Colombo to Dubai (4h), Singapore (3h45), London (10h30), and Indian cities. Low-cost carriers (IndiGo, AirAsia) offer affordable connections through regional hubs. The tourist rail network is an attraction in itself — buy train tickets in advance at Fort Railway Station or online.

## Getting Around
**Trains** — the most scenic and atmospheric way to travel; the Kandy–Ella section (6–7 hours through tea country) is frequently cited as one of the world's great train journeys. Book 1st-class "observation saloon" seats weeks ahead.
**Buses** — the cheapest option; air-conditioned express buses between major cities are comfortable; local buses are very cheap (LKR 50–200) but crowded.
**Tuk-tuks** — ubiquitous for short-distance city and resort travel; negotiate fares or use PickMe (Sri Lanka's Grab equivalent).
**Rental car with driver** — excellent value (USD 40–70/day); enables maximum flexibility especially for remote sites and national parks.
**Domestic flights** — FitsAir connects Colombo to Jaffna, Batticaloa, and Trincomalee; useful for reaching the north and east coast.

## Cultural Triangle
Sri Lanka's ancient civilisation left an extraordinary monumental heritage in the dry-zone north-centre:
- **Anuradhapura** — one of Asia's oldest continuously inhabited cities (4th century BCE–10th century CE); massive dagobas (white-domed Buddhist stupas), the sacred Sri Maha Bodhi tree (claimed to be the world's oldest known planted tree, 288 BCE), and the Ruwanwelisaya stupa. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Polonnaruwa** — medieval capital (10th–13th century CE); the Gal Vihara rock sculptures (four enormous Buddha figures carved into a single granite outcrop) are among the greatest works of Buddhist art in the world. UNESCO.
- **Sigiriya** — 5th-century rock fortress rising 200 m above the jungle plain; ancient frescoes of "cloud maidens," mirror walls with 1,200-year-old graffiti, and the summit with its lion-paw entrance gateway. UNESCO. Sigiriya remains the most spectacular single site in Sri Lanka.
- **Dambulla Cave Temple** — five cave temples with over 150 Buddha statues and 2,100 m² of paintings; UNESCO.

## Kandy and the Hill Country
- **Kandy** — the last royal capital of Sri Lanka; the Temple of the Tooth Relic (sacred tooth of Buddha; puja ceremony at 6:30/9:30 am and 6:30 pm) is Sri Lanka's holiest site.
- **Peradeniya Royal Botanical Gardens** — 60 hectares of tropical gardens; the avenue of royal palms and the giant bamboo are memorable.
- **Tea country (Nuwara Eliya, Ella)** — misty highlands at 1,800+ m; tea plantations cover the hillsides; factory tours explain the processing from leaf to cup; temperatures are refreshingly cool.
- **Ella** — the most popular hill-country town; the Nine Arch Bridge (a colonial railway viaduct through tea fields, photographed as trains cross at dawn), Little Adam's Peak (easy hike with great views), and the start of the Horton Plains World's End walk.
- **Horton Plains National Park** — 3,160 m high plateau; sambar deer, endemic birds, and the "World's End" cliff with a 900 m drop.

## Wildlife
- **Yala National Park** — highest leopard density in the world; elephants, sloth bears, mugger crocodiles, and hundreds of bird species. The park is heavily visited; consider Udawalawe (better for elephants, less crowded) or Wilpattu (best for leopards, completely uncrowded).
- **Udawalawe National Park** — 500+ resident elephants; the Elephant Transit Home near the park releases orphaned elephants.
- **Minneriya National Park** — "The Gathering"; June–October sees 200–300 wild elephants congregating around the ancient reservoir; one of Asia's great wildlife spectacles.
- **Sinharaja Forest Reserve** — UNESCO Biosphere Reserve; Sri Lanka's last significant lowland rainforest; endemic bird species; guided walks required.

## Beaches
- **Mirissa** — small, crescent-shaped bay; whale watching from November–April (blue whales, sperm whales); good surf for beginners; low-key beach bar scene.
- **Unawatuna** — crescent bay near Galle; popular and slightly crowded; good snorkelling.
- **Arugam Bay** — world-class right-hand surf break; September–December peak; mellow surf culture; remote eastern coast.
- **Nilaveli and Trincomalee** — northeast coast; pristine beaches; Pigeon Island for snorkelling; fewer tourists than the south.
- **Tangalle** — long, wild beaches; more isolation than the south-coast resort towns.

## Galle Fort
The Portuguese/Dutch colonial fortified city on the southwest coast is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The 36-hectare Dutch fort (built 1663) contains a living city of guesthouses, restaurants, boutiques, and colonial architecture within its ramparts. Walking the ramparts at sunset is a highlight. The interior streets are peaceful and photogenic.

## Eat and Drink
Sri Lankan cuisine is underrated globally — intensely spiced, coconut-rich, and diverse:
- **Rice and curry** — the daily staple; 8–12 separate curries (fish, chicken, dhal, jackfruit, pappadam, sambol) served with rice on a banana leaf; USD 2–4 at local restaurants.
- **Hoppers (appa)** — bowl-shaped crispy rice-flour pancakes; eaten for breakfast with egg and sambol.
- **Kottu roti** — shredded flatbread stir-fried with vegetables, egg, and meat; the street-food soundtrack (rhythmic chopping) is inescapable.
- **Pol sambol** — fresh coconut relish with chilli and lime; accompanies almost every meal.
- **Ceylon tea** — arguably the world's finest; the hill-country plantation teas (Nuwara Eliya, Dimbula, Uva) are complex and distinct.
- **Arrack** — coconut palm spirit; the national liquor; best mixed with ginger beer.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR); 1 USD ≈ LKR 305 (rates stabilised after 2022 crisis).
- **Language**: Sinhala and Tamil; English widely spoken in tourist areas.
- **Safety**: Generally safe after peace was restored; roads are the primary hazard.
- **Best time to visit**: December–April for the south and west; May–September for the east coast (Arugam Bay). The island has two monsoon seasons and it's always sunny somewhere.
- **Budget**: Excellent value; USD 40–80/day mid-range. Luxury boutique hotels are exceptional value (USD 100–200/night) by Asian standards.
- **Visa**: eTA (electronic travel authorisation) required; apply online before travel.
