## New Zealand (South Island) — Destination Guide

New Zealand's South Island is one of the world's great adventure and wilderness destinations: Fiordland's fjords, the Southern Alps, Queenstown's adrenaline scene, Aoraki/Mount Cook, and the turquoise lakes of the Mackenzie Basin. It is also home to some of the world's finest long-distance walking (the Great Walks) and a wine region (Marlborough) that produces the world's benchmark Sauvignon Blanc.

## Overview

The South Island covers 150,437 km². Key regions:
- **Queenstown:** The world's adventure capital; bungee jumping, jet boating, skiing, Lord of the Rings landscapes.
- **Fiordland National Park:** Milford Sound, Doubtful Sound; UNESCO World Heritage rainforest fjords.
- **Aoraki/Mount Cook National Park:** New Zealand's highest peak (3,724m), Hooker Valley glacier walks.
- **Marlborough:** Sauvignon Blanc wine region; the Marlborough Sounds.
- **Abel Tasman National Park:** Golden sand beaches, clear water, kayaking, the Abel Tasman Coast Track.
- **West Coast:** Pancake Rocks (Punakaiki), Franz Josef and Fox Glaciers, the untouched rainforest.
- **Dunedin:** Victorian architecture, Otago Peninsula (penguins, albatross, sea lions).

## Best Time to Visit

**December–March (Southern Hemisphere summer):** Long days, best weather for tramping and outdoor activities. Queenstown is spectacular; Great Walk bookings fill up months ahead. January temperatures: Queenstown 22°C, Christchurch 23°C.

**June–September (winter):** Ski season. Queenstown and Wanaka ski fields (Coronet Peak, Cardrona, Treble Cone) are excellent; $90–120/day lift pass. Fiordland receives 6–8m of annual rainfall (among the world's highest) and is beautiful in any season.

**April–May (autumn):** Best foliage, fewer tourists, mild temperatures, lower accommodation prices. October–November (spring) is also excellent.

## Getting In

Christchurch (CHC) and Queenstown (ZQN) are the main South Island gateways. International connections: Sydney (3.5 hr), Melbourne (3 hr), Singapore (10 hr), Dubai (17 hr), Los Angeles (12 hr). Air New Zealand is the primary carrier. From Australia: $200–400 return; from UK: $900–1,400 return.

## Getting Around

A rental car is virtually mandatory for the South Island. Freedom camping is partially permitted but requires a self-contained vehicle. Rental: $40–80/day for a standard car; campervans: $80–150/day (hire from Jucy, Maui, Britz). Gas: NZD $2.70/litre ($1.65 USD). Roads are excellent; some West Coast and Fiordland roads are single-lane.

**Internal flights:** Christchurch–Queenstown: 45 minutes, $80–150. Useful for avoiding the 8-hour drive.

**Interislander ferry:** From Wellington (North Island) to Picton (Marlborough): 3.5 hours, $50–120.

## Costs

New Zealand is moderately expensive. Budget: $80–110/day. Mid-range: $130–200/day.

- Coffee (flat white — New Zealand's contribution to global coffee culture): NZD $5–6 ($3–3.70)
- Café lunch: NZD $18–25 ($11–15)
- Restaurant dinner: NZD $35–70 ($21–43) per person
- Hostel (dorm): NZD $30–45 ($18–28)/night

## Great Walks (Tramping)

The Department of Conservation (DOC) manages nine Great Walks; five are on the South Island. All require advance booking (greatewalks.doc.govt.nz, opens early May for the following season).

**Milford Track (4 days):** Widely considered the finest walk in the world. 53.5 km through Fiordland; hut bookings include breakfast and dinner ($180/person). 14,000 people do it each season — it books out in minutes in May.

**Routeburn Track (2–3 days):** Between Fiordland and Mount Aspiring NP; diverse scenery including the views from Key Summit. $100/person/night in huts.

**Abel Tasman Coast Track (3–5 days):** The country's most popular Great Walk; golden beaches, sea kayaking, clear water. Sea shuttle connections allow flexible point-to-point hiking.

## Fiordland

**Milford Sound:** The most visited destination in NZ. A 2.5-hour road from Te Anau passes through the Homer Tunnel. Morning cruises (7am: $85–95) are calmer and clearer before daily rain clouds build. Overnight cruises ($300–450) include a night in the silence of the fjord.

**Doubtful Sound:** Three times as long as Milford, less visited, reached by boat and bus via Lake Manapouri. Overnight expedition: $250–400. Fiordland bottlenose dolphins, fur seals, rare fiordland crested penguins.

## Queenstown Activities

- **AJ Hackett Bungy (Kawarau Bridge):** The world's first commercial bungee site (1988). NZD $230.
- **Shotover Jet:** Jet boat through narrow rock canyons. NZD $149.
- **Skydive over the Remarkables:** From 15,000 ft. NZD $329.
- **Remarkables ski field:** 30 minutes from town; excellent beginner and intermediate terrain.
- **Milford Sound day tour from Queenstown:** 12-hour coach and cruise; NZD $150–220.

## Practical Tips

- Sand flies (Fiordland): The most persistent biting insects in the Southern Hemisphere. 100% DEET or Bushman insect repellent is not optional in Fiordland; they bite through light clothing.
- Drive on the left; roads are narrower than expected. Rental cars come with NZ road code; read it.
- Book Great Walks the moment bookings open in May; popular tracks book out in hours.
- Responsible camping: Leave No Trace is taken seriously; NZ's environment is fragile.
- Tipping: Not a strong culture; rounding up is appreciated but 10–15% tips are uncommon.
