# Dubai, United Arab Emirates

## Overview
Dubai is a dazzling city-state in the UAE that rose from desert sands to become one of the world's premier luxury travel destinations in just 50 years. It holds the world's tallest building, largest shopping mall, and most ambitious artificial islands. Despite its reputation for excess, Dubai also offers genuine cultural heritage, excellent food, and access to remarkable desert landscapes.

## Getting There
Dubai International Airport (DXB) is the world's busiest international airport by passenger traffic, with direct connections to virtually every major city. Emirates, flydubai, and dozens of global carriers serve the hub. Al Maktoum International Airport (DWC) handles some budget carriers. The Dubai Metro Red Line connects DXB to the city centre in 30 minutes for AED 8–12.

## Getting Around
The Dubai Metro (Red and Green lines) is clean, air-conditioned, and covers major tourist corridors including the Mall of the Emirates, Dubai Mall, and Burj Khalifa. Taxis are plentiful, metered, and affordable by Western standards (starting fare AED 5). Uber and Careem operate widely. Driving is feasible but traffic during rush hour is brutal. The Palm Monorail connects the Palm Jumeirah.

## Neighbourhoods and Areas
**Downtown Dubai** — Burj Khalifa, Dubai Fountain, Dubai Mall; the modern heart of the city.
**Dubai Marina** — yacht-lined waterway, beachfront restaurants, The Walk promenade, and numerous rooftop bars.
**Jumeirah Beach Residence (JBR)** — outdoor shopping and dining promenade directly on the beach.
**Deira and Bur Dubai** — the historic core; gold and spice souks, abra water taxis across Dubai Creek, and the Dubai Museum.
**Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood (Al Bastakiya)** — wind-tower houses, art galleries, and the Coffee Museum; the city's most authentic quarter.
**Palm Jumeirah** — artificial island hosting the iconic Atlantis resort and ultra-luxury hotels.
**Dubai Hills and Jumeirah Golf Estates** — residential luxury with world-class golf courses.

## See
- **Burj Khalifa** — 828-m tower; At the Top observation deck on floors 124/125 offers panoramic views. Book in advance online to save on ticket prices.
- **Dubai Creek and Gold Souk** — wander the old spice souk's fragrant alleys, then cross by abra (AED 1) to the Gold Souk, packed with jewellery.
- **The Dubai Frame** — 150-m picture-frame structure offering views of old and new Dubai.
- **Miracle Garden** — 72,000 m² flower garden with over 150 million blooms; open October–April only.
- **Museum of the Future** — stunning torus-shaped building near Sheikh Zayed Road exploring near-future technology and society.
- **Desert Safari** — 4WD dune bashing, camel riding, falconry, and BBQ dinner under stars; easily arranged from any hotel.

## Do
- Ski at Ski Dubai, an indoor ski resort inside the Mall of the Emirates.
- Take a sunset dhow cruise along Dubai Creek or Dubai Marina.
- Experience a hot-air balloon flight over the desert at dawn.
- Visit the Global Village (October–April) — a cultural festival of pavilions from 90+ countries.
- Dive or snorkel at Jumeirah Beach or the artificial reefs off Dubai's coast.
- Watch the Dubai Fountain show from the Burj Khalifa Lake — free and runs every 30 minutes after sunset.

## Eat and Drink
Dubai has a remarkable dining scene reflecting its 200+ nationalities:
- **Arabic/Levantine** — mezze, shawarma, and mansaf; Al Ustad Special Kebab in Deira is an institution.
- **Indian** — enormous Indian expat community means outstanding curries, biryanis, and dosas at every price point.
- **Brunch** — Friday brunch is a Dubai institution; hotel buffets pile on unlimited food, beverages, and entertainment for AED 250–600.
- **Fine dining** — La Petite Maison, Nobu, Zuma, and Nusr-Et (Salt Bae's original) represent the high end.
- **Alcohol** — available in licensed hotels and restaurants, not in shopping malls or on the street; bars close at 3 am.

## Sleep
Dubai excels at luxury hospitality. The Burj Al Arab (the sail-shaped hotel visible from everywhere) is among the world's most famous ultra-luxury properties. Atlantis The Palm, the Bulgari Resort, and One&Only The Palm set the standard. Mid-range options exist in Deira and Bur Dubai (Rove Hotels offer good value). Budget travellers use OYO or smaller hotels near the airport, or stay in Dubai Marina serviced apartments.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: UAE Dirham (AED); 1 USD ≈ 3.67 AED (fixed peg).
- **Language**: Arabic (official); English is widely spoken everywhere.
- **Safety**: Exceptionally safe; strict laws against public intoxication, drug use, and public displays of affection.
- **Best time to visit**: November–March for outdoor activities (25–30°C). Summers (June–September) are brutally hot (40–50°C) but indoor attractions run on reduced prices.
- **Dress code**: Conservative dress required in souks, malls, and government buildings; beachwear only on the beach.
- **Budget**: Luxury-oriented; mid-range spend is USD 150–300/day. Budget options exist but the city rewards spending.
- **Ramadan**: During the holy month, eating/drinking in public during daylight hours is prohibited; many restaurants close; a culturally fascinating time to visit if you respect the customs.
