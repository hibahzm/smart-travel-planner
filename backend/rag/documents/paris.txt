# Paris, France

## Overview
Paris needs little introduction — the City of Light is one of the world's most visited destinations, attracting over 30 million tourists annually. Yet beyond the Eiffel Tower postcards lies a city of unparalleled depth: extraordinary museums, a food culture that shaped the world's culinary vocabulary, and 20 arrondissements each with a distinct character. Paris rewards slow travel and repeated visits.

## Getting There
Paris is served by Charles de Gaulle Airport (CDG), 25 km northeast, and Orly Airport (ORY), 14 km south. CDG connects to the city via RER B train (€12.10, 35 minutes to Gare du Nord), the Roissybus (€16.10), or taxis (€50–65 fixed fare to Right Bank). Orly uses the OrlyBus or Orlyval tram + RER B. Paris is also a major Eurostar terminus (London St Pancras in 2h15), and TGV high-speed trains connect to all major French cities.

## Getting Around
The Paris Métro is one of the world's densest and most frequent metro networks — no point in central Paris is more than 500 m from a station. Buy a carnet of 10 tickets or load a Navigo Découverte weekly pass (€30 for unlimited travel within Zones 1–5). Vélib' bike-share has thousands of docking stations. Taxis exist but are expensive; Uber and Bolt operate. Walking is often the best option — central Paris is compact and exceptionally beautiful on foot.

## Arrondissements to Know
**1st & 4th** — Louvre, Marais district, Centre Pompidou, Île de la Cité (Notre-Dame).
**5th & 6th** — Latin Quarter, Sorbonne, Saint-Germain-des-Prés, Luxembourg Gardens.
**7th** — Eiffel Tower, Musée d'Orsay, Invalides.
**8th** — Champs-Élysées, Arc de Triomphe, luxury shopping (Faubourg Saint-Honoré).
**18th** — Montmartre, Sacré-Cœur Basilica, artist studios, and Pigalle.
**10th/11th** — Canal Saint-Martin, vibrant bars, and the young Parisian scene.
**20th** — Père-Lachaise cemetery and the Belleville multicultural neighbourhood.

## See
- **Louvre Museum** — the world's largest art museum; home to the Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, and 35,000 other works. Book timed entry online.
- **Musée d'Orsay** — the world's greatest Impressionist collection, housed in a magnificent Beaux-Arts railway station.
- **Eiffel Tower** — summit access requires advance booking months ahead in peak season; the second-floor terrace offers excellent views without the queues.
- **Notre-Dame de Paris** — the Gothic cathedral, rebuilt after the 2019 fire, reopened in December 2024.
- **Centre Pompidou** — world-class modern and contemporary art in an iconic inside-out building.
- **Sainte-Chapelle** — exquisite 13th-century Gothic chapel with floor-to-ceiling stained-glass windows; often overlooked.
- **Palais Royal** — colonnaded garden and Daniel Buren's striped columns; free and rarely crowded.
- **Versailles** — a 40-minute RER C ride; the palace and gardens require a full day.

## Do
- Spend a morning at the Marché d'Aligre (12th) or Marché des Enfants Rouges (3rd, Paris's oldest covered market).
- Stroll the Canal Saint-Martin on a Sunday when the road is closed to cars.
- Attend a performance at the Opéra Garnier or Opéra Bastille.
- Visit the Catacombs — 300 km of underground tunnels housing the bones of six million Parisians.
- Rent a Vélib' and cycle along the Seine to the Bois de Boulogne.
- Take an evening Seine cruise (Bateaux Mouches) when the city is illuminated.

## Eat and Drink
French cuisine is a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, and Paris is its spiritual home:
- **Brasseries** — steak-frites, onion soup, croque-monsieur; classics done properly at Brasserie Lipp, Bofinger, or La Coupole.
- **Bistros** — smaller, cheaper, more personal; look for a chalked-up plat du jour (€12–18).
- **Boulangeries** — a freshly baked croissant with café au lait is the Parisian breakfast archetype.
- **Patisseries** — Pierre Hermé for macarons; Ladurée for history; Cyril Lignac for croissants.
- **Natural wine bars** — Le Verre Volé, Septime La Cave, and Frenchie Bar à Vins represent the new Paris.
- **Fine dining** — Guy Savoy, Alain Ducasse au Plaza Athénée, and Septime are among the city's three-star destinations.
- **Picnics** — buy cheese at a fromagerie, charcuterie at a traiteur, bread at a boulangerie, and wine at a Nicolas; eat by the Seine or in any park.

## Sleep
Paris has accommodation across all budgets. Luxury landmarks include the Ritz, Le Bristol, and the Four Seasons George V. Mid-range: boutique hotels in the Marais or 11th arrondissement offer style from €150–250/night. Budget: hostels in the 18th (Montmartre) and near Gare du Nord. Note that Haussmann buildings have no lifts and rooms can be small; check before booking.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR).
- **Language**: French; English is spoken in tourist areas but a few words of French go a long way.
- **Safety**: Generally safe; pickpocketing is common near major monuments and on the Métro. Keep bags closed and phones off tables.
- **Best time to visit**: April–June (spring) and September–October (autumn) for mild weather and smaller crowds. August sees Parisians leave and some businesses close.
- **Budget**: Mid-range spend €120–200/day including accommodation, meals, and entry fees.
- **Museum Pass**: The Paris Museum Pass covers 50+ sites including the Louvre, Versailles, and Orsay; excellent value for 2+ days of sightseeing.
