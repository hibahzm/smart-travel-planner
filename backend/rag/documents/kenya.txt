## Kenya — Destination Guide

Kenya is East Africa's most accessible safari destination, combining the world's greatest wildlife spectacle (the Masai Mara wildebeest migration), the snow-capped equatorial peak of Mount Kenya (5,199m), Indian Ocean beaches at Diani and Lamu, and a capital, Nairobi, that is increasingly cosmopolitan and well-connected internationally.

## Overview

Kenya covers 580,367 km². Key regions:
- **Masai Mara National Reserve:** The Kenyan side of the Serengeti ecosystem; the greatest concentration of large mammals on earth.
- **Amboseli National Park:** Elephant herds under Kilimanjaro.
- **Samburu National Reserve:** Rare northern species (reticulated giraffe, Grevy's zebra, Beisa oryx).
- **Mount Kenya:** Africa's second-highest peak; Point Lenana (4,985m) is the trekker's summit; technical routes to Batian (5,199m) for climbers.
- **Diani Beach (Mombasa Coast):** White coral sand beaches, excellent snorkelling, whale sharks.
- **Lamu Archipelago:** Swahili culture, dhow sailing, no cars.
- **Nairobi:** Giraffe Centre, Sheldrick Elephant Orphanage, vibrant food scene.

## Best Time to Visit

**July–October (dry season):** The best time for wildlife. July–September is peak wildebeest river crossing season at the Masai Mara — the world's greatest natural spectacle. Dry, dusty, warm days; cool nights. Book lodges 12 months ahead for peak crossing weeks (late July–September).

**January–February (short dry season):** The second dry season. Excellent game viewing in Amboseli. Fewer tourists than July–October. Calving season at Masai Mara — lion and cheetah activity highest.

**March–June (long rains):** The landscape is green and beautiful; birds are spectacular. Prices drop 30–50%. Some dirt roads become impassable; the Mara migration is absent. Good for Nairobi and coast.

## Getting In

Nairobi Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (NBO) is the region's major hub. Connections from London (8 hr, British Airways, Kenya Airways), Amsterdam, Dubai, Addis Ababa, and Johannesburg. Nairobi Wilson Airport (WIL) is the domestic/charter hub for light aircraft safaris. Flying between parks is the norm for high-end safari itineraries; charter flights: $200–400/person/sector.

## Safari Options

**Self-drive:** Possible in the bigger parks (Amboseli, Tsavo) with a 4WD. Not recommended for Masai Mara (roads require experience; you need a guide for tracking). Hire a 4WD Land Cruiser with pop-up roof in Nairobi: $100–150/day plus fuel.

**Mid-range safari (lodge-based):** All-inclusive safari lodges: $200–400/person/night including game drives, meals, and park fees. Operators: Gamewatchers, Private Safaris Kenya, Basecamp Explorer.

**Budget camping safari:** Camping inside park (designated sites: $25–40/night plus park fees) with a tour operator: group camping safaris from Nairobi, 3 days/2 nights to Masai Mara: $300–450/person.

**High-end (tented camps):** Wilderness camps like Angama Mara, Cottar's 1920s Camp, or Mahali Mzuri: $700–1,500/person/night. All-inclusive, fewer than 20 guests, unmatched access and service.

## Park Fees

Kenya's national park fees are paid via the M-Pesa or online system (eCitizen portal):
- Masai Mara National Reserve: $100/adult/day (non-residents) — paid directly to Narok County
- Amboseli National Park: $90/adult/day
- Note: the Mara conservancies (Olare Motorogi, Naboisho, Ol Kinyei) charge additional conservancy fees ($90–120/day) but offer exclusive zones with no other vehicles.

## Wildlife

The Masai Mara is one of the world's last intact savannah ecosystems. Year-round residents include lions (the Mara's pride system is well-documented), leopards, cheetahs, elephants, buffalo, hippos, and crocodiles. The wildebeest migration (1.5 million animals plus 200,000 zebra and gazelle) arrives from Tanzania's Serengeti in July and returns southward October–November. River crossings at the Mara River are the key event — they are unpredictable but guides track them daily.

## Costs

- Budget camping safari (3 days, Masai Mara): $300–450 per person
- Mid-range safari lodge (per person per night): $250–500
- Luxury tented camp (per person per night): $700–1,500
- Nairobi airport to city centre: $20–30 (Uber/taxi)
- Café/restaurant meal in Nairobi: $8–20
- Nyama choma (roast meat) at a local restaurant: $6–12

## Practical Tips

- Yellow fever vaccination certificate required if arriving from a yellow-fever-endemic country (or transiting through one).
- Malaria: Kenya is high-risk. Take prophylaxis (malarone or doxycycline); start before arrival. Consult a travel clinic.
- Mobile money: M-Pesa is used for almost everything; load a local SIM (Safaricom) on arrival (KES 100 for a SIM). Park fees, supermarkets, taxis all accept M-Pesa.
- Diani Beach: 90 minutes south of Mombasa, excellent for a beach end to a safari itinerary. Turtle Bay and Almanara resorts.
- Photography: The morning light (6–9am) and evening light (4–7pm) are dramatically better than midday; plan game drives around golden hour.
