## Vietnam — Destination Guide

Vietnam is a 1,650 km-long S-shaped country stretching from the Red River Delta in the north to the Mekong Delta in the south. It is simultaneously one of Asia's best food destinations, one of its most affordable countries, and a landscape of extraordinary variety: rice terraces in the north, karst limestone in Ha Long Bay and Ninh Bình, imperial history in Huế, colonial architecture in Hoi An, and beaches along 3,260 km of coastline.

## Overview

The country divides roughly into three regions: the North (Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Sapa, Ha Giang), the Centre (Huế, Hoi An, Da Nang), and the South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong Delta, Phu Quoc). A full north-to-south trip requires a minimum of 3 weeks; most visitors choose one region or use domestic flights to combine two.

## Best Time to Visit

Vietnam's climate is regional — there is no single best time for the whole country.

**North (Hanoi, Sapa):** Best October–April (dry, cooler). May–August is hot and humid (35°C+); June–September is typhoon season. Sapa's rice terraces peak September–November (harvest season) and April–May (planting, vivid green).

**Centre (Hoi An, Huế, Da Nang):** Best February–May (dry, warm). October–December brings heavy rain (central Vietnam faces the South China Sea and catches typhoons September–December). The Hoi An lantern festival falls on the full moon of every month — the most atmospheric is in February.

**South (HCMC, Mekong, Phu Quoc):** Best November–April (dry season). May–October is wet; Phu Quoc beaches are cloudy and rough July–September.

## Getting In

Hanoi Noi Bai (HAN) and Ho Chi Minh City Tan Son Nhat (SGN) are the main international airports. Major carriers (Vietnam Airlines, VietJet, Bamboo Airways, Thai Airways, Singapore Airlines) connect from regional hubs. Budget fares from Bangkok: $30–60. From Singapore: $50–100. From London/Paris: $600–900 return. Vietnam e-visa: $25 USD, 90 days, easily obtained online at evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn.

## Getting Around

**Domestic flights:** Vietnam's distances make flying practical. VietJet and Bamboo Airways offer Hanoi–HCMC from $25–60. Book in advance.

**Trains (Vietnam Railways):** The Reunification Express runs the length of the country. Hanoi to Hoi An (Da Nang): 17 hours overnight, from $25 soft sleeper. Beautiful coastal section from Da Nang to Hue. Book at baolau.com or dsvn.vn.

**Sleeper buses:** Cheaper than trains for many routes. Open-tour "hop-on" bus tickets (Hanoi to HCMC, stop anywhere): $30–40. Reliable operators: Futa Bus, The Sinh Tourist.

**Motorbike rental:** $5–10/day in most cities. An international driving permit is required; checkpoints exist. The "Easy Rider" motorbike tours in Dalat and Ha Giang use local drivers as guides: $20–30/day, highly recommended.

## Costs

Vietnam is excellent budget value. Budget: $20–35/day. Mid-range: $50–80/day. Luxury: $150–300/day.

- Phở in a local restaurant: 40,000–70,000 VND ($1.60–2.80)
- Bánh mì from a street stall: 20,000–40,000 VND ($0.80–1.60)
- Street food meal: 50,000–100,000 VND ($2–4)
- Mid-range restaurant: 150,000–300,000 VND ($6–12)
- Guesthouse/hostel (private room): 150,000–350,000 VND ($6–14)/night
- Mid-range hotel: 600,000–1,200,000 VND ($24–48)/night

## Top Experiences

**Ha Long Bay:** 1,969 islands of karst limestone rising from emerald water. An overnight cruise (2 days/1 night) is essential for sunset and sunrise views and kayaking into caves. Quality ranges wildly: budget junk boats ($60–80/person) to luxury cruises ($200–400). Avoid the cheapest boats — they anchor in the crowded main bay. Mid-range operators (Heritage Line, Indochina Junk) anchor in quieter areas.

**Hoi An Ancient Town:** A UNESCO-protected 16th-century trading port with 800+ heritage buildings. The tailors are the main draw: custom suits in 24 hours for $80–150 (quality varies; use tailors with English-speaking staff and see samples). Lantern festival on the full moon: streets lit with paper lanterns, electric lights extinguished. Cooking classes: $25–40, highly recommended.

**Sapa (Northwest Vietnam):** Hill-tribe villages, terraced rice paddies, and Fansipan (3,143m, highest peak in Indochina). The cable car to the summit costs $25; the 2-day trek to the summit is $50–80 including guide and homestay. Market days (Saturday, Bac Ha; Sunday, Coc Ly) are the best for hill-tribe textiles.

**Ho Chi Minh City (Saigon):** The chaotic, ambitious south. The War Remnants Museum is harrowing and essential. The Cu Chi Tunnels (Viet Cong tunnel network, 1.5 hr from the city) cost $12. The night market in District 1 (Bui Vien Street) is one of Southeast Asia's most energetic street scenes.

**Ha Giang Loop:** The most spectacular motorbike route in Vietnam. 4–5 days through the Dong Van Karst Plateau Geopark, Ma Pi Leng Pass, and Hmong villages. Rent a motorbike in Ha Giang (~$8/day) or join an Easy Rider guide. Not for beginner riders — roads are mountain switchbacks above deep gorges.

## Practical Tips

- Currency: Vietnamese Dong (VND). 1 USD ≈ 25,000 VND. ATMs are abundant; BIDV and Vietcombank charge the lowest fees. Carry small bills for street food.
- Visa: The e-visa process is straightforward online. Do NOT use third-party "visa agents" that charge $60–80 — the government site charges $25.
- Scooter safety: Helmets are mandatory. Wear one even for short rides. Hospital treatment for foreigners requires payment upfront; travel insurance with medical evacuation is essential.
- Bargaining: Expected at markets. Starting offer: 30–40% of asking price.
