## Portugal — Destination Guide

Portugal punches far above its weight as a travel destination: world-class food (pastéis de nata, grilled fish, alentejano pork), extraordinary wine (port, vinho verde, Douro valley reds), a history that spans Romans, Moors, and a global maritime empire, Atlantic beaches from the Algarve to Nazaré (home of the world's biggest surfable waves), and one of Western Europe's most affordable costs of living. It is consistently voted the world's best tourism destination.

## Overview

Portugal covers 92,212 km² on the Iberian Peninsula's western edge. Key regions:
- **Lisbon:** The hilly capital on the Tagus estuary, famous for trams, viewpoints (miradouros), fado music, and pastéis de Belém.
- **Porto:** Northern industrial city turned creative hub; port wine lodges line the Douro riverfront.
- **Algarve:** Southern coast, 150 km of golden limestone cliffs and beaches; Europe's golf capital.
- **Douro Valley:** UNESCO-listed terraced vineyards along the Douro River; wine tourism and river cruises.
- **Sintra:** Fairy-tale palaces and quintas in a forested hill 40 minutes from Lisbon.
- **Alentejo:** Cork forests, medieval walled towns, whitewashed villages, excellent wines.

## Best Time to Visit

**May–June and September–October:** The sweet spot. Temperatures 20–28°C, low humidity, fewer crowds than July–August, accommodation 20–40% cheaper. Lisbon in June hosts the Santos Populares (city-wide street party, June 12–13) — one of Europe's greatest free festivals.

**July–August:** Peak season. The Algarve is excellent (30–35°C, guaranteed sun) but crowded and expensive. Lisbon is hot (35°C+) and overwhelmed with tourists. Porto is slightly cooler.

**November–March:** Off-season. Surfing season on the west coast (Nazaré giants October–February, Ericeira surf-able year-round). Lisbon in winter is mild (12–17°C), atmospheric, and quiet. The Algarve nearly empties; prices drop 50%.

## Getting In

Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) is the main hub. Porto Sá Carneiro (OPO) is increasingly well-connected. Both served by TAP Portugal, Ryanair, easyJet, and British Airways from major European cities for $30–150 return from London; $400–700 from New York. The airport metro to central Lisbon: 30 minutes, €1.65 ($1.80).

## Getting Around

**Trains (CP Comboios de Portugal):** Lisbon–Porto: 3 hours direct, €25–35 ($27–38). Book online at cp.pt. Regional trains serve smaller towns; they are slow but beautiful (Douro line from Porto is world-class scenic).

**Inter-city buses (Rede Expressos, Flixbus):** Cover destinations trains don't reach. Lisbon–Faro (Algarve): 3.5 hr, €20.

**Car rental:** Essential for the Algarve, Alentejo, and Douro. Fuel: ~€1.70/litre ($1.85). Roads are good; parking in cities is challenging and expensive.

**Trams in Lisbon:** Tram 28 is the most scenic but extremely crowded with tourists. The 12E and 18E are used by locals and equally scenic. Single ticket: €3.10 ($3.40); 24-hour card (bus + tram + metro): €6.90 ($7.50).

## Costs

Portugal is Western Europe's best value. Budget: $60–80/day. Mid-range: $100–160/day. Luxury: $300–600+/day.

- Pastel de nata at Pastéis de Belém (the original, founded 1837): €1.30 ($1.40) each
- Wine at a restaurant: from €3 ($3.30)/glass; bottle from €12 ($13) in a good restaurant
- Grilled fish (peixe grelhado) lunch with wine: €12–20 ($13–22) per person
- Mid-range dinner in Lisbon: €25–40 ($27–44) per person
- Hostel (private room): €50–80 ($55–87)/night
- Mid-range hotel (3-star): €80–150 ($87–163)/night in Lisbon; €60–120 ($65–130) in Porto

## Top Experiences

**Lisbon Alfama and Miradouros:** The Moorish quarter winds up to the São Jorge Castle. At sunset, the Miradouro da Graça and Miradouro de Santa Catarina fill with locals drinking Super Bock and watching the light turn gold on the Tagus. Fado houses: A Baiuca (small, authentic, Alfama) and Tasca do Chico (best fado in Lisbon, book weeks ahead, €25 minimum spend).

**Sintra:** 45-minute train from Lisbon Rossio (€2.25 return). The Pena Palace (€14, book online), the Moorish Castle (€8), and the secret Quinta da Regaleira (€10) occupy a full day. Go on a weekday — weekends are overwhelmingly busy.

**Porto and Port Wine:** The Ribeira riverfront is UNESCO-listed; Vila Nova de Gaia on the opposite bank has the port wine lodges (Graham's, Sandeman, Ramos Pinto). Tours: €15–20 including 2 tastings. The LNP (local wine bar, Rua das Flores) has the best port wine list in the city.

**Algarve:** The Benagil Sea Cave (accessible only by kayak or swimming — book a guided kayak tour, €30–40, at 8am before it crowds) is one of Europe's most beautiful natural arches. Lagos has the finest beaches. Sagres at the southwestern tip feels like the edge of the world.

**Douro Valley:** River cruise from Porto upstream past terraced vineyards: full-day tour from €80. Quinta das Carvalhas and Quinta do Crasto offer wine tastings with valley views for €15–25.

## Practical Tips

- Tax refund (VIES): Non-EU visitors can claim VAT back (23%) on purchases over €50. Get the form at point of sale; stamp at the airport.
- The Portuguese are formal in initial encounters; address people as "Senhor/Senhora" in shops and restaurants.
- Tipping: 5–10% at restaurants if service was good; not expected at cafés.
- Safety: Portugal is one of Europe's safest countries. Pickpocketing in tourist areas (tram 28, Alfama) is the main concern.
