## Bali, Indonesia — Destination Guide

Bali is Indonesia's most internationally known island, a Hindu-majority enclave in a Muslim-majority nation. It is simultaneously a surfer's paradise, a spiritual retreat, and a cultural treasure. Despite growing overtourism in Kuta and Seminyak, the island's interior — Ubud, Sidemen, Amed — remains genuinely beautiful and surprisingly affordable.

## Overview

Bali covers 5,780 km² and sits between Java to the west and Lombok to the east. The population of approximately 4 million is predominantly Balinese Hindu, and religious ceremonies, offerings (canang sari), and temple festivals are woven into daily life. You will encounter them constantly; they are not tourist performances.

The island divides into distinct zones: the beach resort strip (Kuta/Legian/Seminyak/Canggu) on the west coast; the cultural and creative heartland around Ubud in the central highlands; the quieter east coast (Amed, Candidasa) with excellent diving; and the calmer north (Lovina) away from package tourism.

## Best Time to Visit

The dry season runs May through September — this is when Bali is at its most pleasant. July and August are peak months; book accommodation months in advance and expect crowds at Ubud's Monkey Forest and the Tanah Lot temple. The wet season (November through March) brings heavy afternoon rain but also lush green rice terraces, half the crowds, and prices 20–40% lower. April and October are shoulder months offering the best balance.

## Getting In

**By air:** Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) is the hub. Direct flights connect from Singapore (1.5 hr), Kuala Lumpur (2 hr), Sydney (6 hr), and Tokyo (6 hr). Budget carriers AirAsia, Lion Air, and Scoot offer sub-$100 fares from Southeast Asian hubs. A Blue Bird taxi from the airport to Seminyak costs around IDR 150,000–200,000 (~$10–13 USD). Reject all touts at the arrivals hall — use the official metered counter.

**By boat from Lombok:** Fast boats (2 hr, ~$25) connect Padangbai (east Bali) to Gili Islands and Lombok regularly.

## Getting Around

Scooter rental is IDR 80,000–100,000 per day (~$5–6 USD). An international driving permit is technically required but rarely checked outside of police checkpoints. Ride apps Gojek and Grab operate throughout the south; they are cheaper than taxis and fare estimates are shown upfront. For day trips, charter a car with driver for IDR 600,000–800,000 (~$40–50 USD) for a full day — worth it for Kintamani or temple circuits.

## Costs

Bali suits budgets from $30/day (budget guesthouse, warungs, scooter) to $500+/day (five-star villa in Seminyak). Realistic mid-range: $60–100/day including accommodation, food, transport, and activities.

- Street food (nasi goreng, mie goreng) at a warung: IDR 20,000–40,000 ($1.20–2.50)
- Mid-range dinner in Ubud: IDR 100,000–200,000 ($6–13)
- Private pool villa Seminyak (per couple): $80–250/night
- Cooking class Ubud: $35–50

## Top Activities

**Ubud:** The cultural capital. Visit the Sacred Monkey Forest, Tegalalang Rice Terraces (best at 7am before the tour buses), and the Ubud Palace for Kecak dance performances at sunset. The Ubud Market opens at 6am for the freshest produce and crafts; prices double by 9am.

**Surfing:** Kuta Beach for beginners (consistent 1–2m waves); Uluwatu, Padang Padang, and Bingin for intermediate–advanced surfers. Surf lessons: ~$20/session including board.

**Diving and snorkelling:** Amed on the east coast is the best value — visibility 20–25m, coral walls, and the USAT Liberty shipwreck in Tulamben ($35–50 for a two-dive trip). Nusa Penida (accessible by fast boat, 45 min, ~$15) offers manta ray encounters at Manta Point.

**Temple circuit:** Tanah Lot (sea temple, dramatic at sunset but very crowded), Uluwatu (clifftop temple, Kecak fire dance at 6pm), Besakih (the "Mother Temple" on Mount Agung). Admission is IDR 50,000–100,000 per temple; a sarong is required and can be borrowed free.

**Mount Batur trek:** Active volcano, 1717m. Guided sunrise trek departs 2am, summit at sunrise. Cost: $35–60 including guide and breakfast. Book via your accommodation; avoid street touts who inflate prices.

## Practical Tips

- Exchange USD at money changers in Kuta/Seminyak (avoid airport). Rate in 2024: ~IDR 15,600/USD. Count your money before leaving the counter; skimming scams exist.
- ATMs: use bank ATMs inside malls; street ATMs add IDR 50,000+ surcharges.
- Nyepi (Balinese New Year, March/April): the entire island shuts for 24 hours — no flights, no traffic, no noise. DPS airport closes. Do not book travel on this date.
- Rabies exists. If bitten by an animal, seek treatment within hours.
- The "Bali belly" is real — stick to cooked food from busy warungs in the first few days.
