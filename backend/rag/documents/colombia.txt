## Colombia — Destination Guide

Colombia is South America's most improved destination: a country that transformed its reputation from its 1990s association with cartel violence into one of the continent's most vibrant, creative, and welcoming travel experiences. Medellín was once the world's most dangerous city; today it has a world-class metro, innovative urban architecture, and a thriving café culture. Cartagena's walled colonial city is among the Americas' most beautiful. The coffee region (Eje Cafetero) is a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of green valleys and colourful jeeps.

## Overview

Key destinations:
- **Medellín:** The City of Eternal Spring (average 24°C year-round); urban innovation, cable cars, Botero Plaza, flower festival (Feria de las Flores, August).
- **Cartagena:** Walled colonial city on the Caribbean; the most photogenic city in South America.
- **Bogotá:** High-altitude capital (2,600m); world-class museums, café culture, Zona Rosa.
- **Coffee Region (Eje Cafetero):** Salento, the Cocora Valley (wax palms — Colombia's national tree), and the world's best coffee.
- **Tayrona National Park:** Caribbean coast, jungle-to-beach trails, indigenous Kogi communities.
- **San Andrés and Providencia:** Caribbean islands, turquoise water, reggaeton culture.

## Best Time to Visit

Colombia is year-round due to its equatorial location, but:
- **December–March and June–August:** Driest periods in most regions. Best for Tayrona (open; closed September–October for ecological recovery), the Coffee Region, and Cartagena.
- **April–May and September–November:** Wet season. Jungle is lush; waterfalls are full; prices are 20–30% lower. Tayrona closes September–October.
- **August:** Medellín's Feria de las Flores — the city's best week; book accommodation 3 months ahead.

## Getting In

Bogotá El Dorado Airport (BOG) is the international hub. Connections from Miami (3 hr), New York (5 hr), Madrid (10 hr), and Lima (3 hr). LATAM, American, Avianca, Copa. Budget fares from Miami: $150–250 return. Medellín Olaya Herrera (EOH, domestic) and Medellín José María Córdova (MDE, international) both serve the city.

## Getting Around

**Domestic flights:** Essential given distances. BOG–MDE: $40–80 (Avianca, Latam, EasyFly). BOG–CTG (Cartagena): $50–90. The jeep (chiva) from Salento to Cocora Valley: 30 minutes, COP 5,000 ($1.25).

**Intercity buses (Berlinas, Flota Magdalena):** Comfortable and safe; Bogotá–Medellín: 8 hours, $20–30. Bogotá–Salento: 8 hours, $25.

**Metro (Medellín):** Excellent; also cable cars (metrocables) connecting hillside comunas to the metro network. Single trip: COP 3,050 ($0.75). The teleférico to Parque Arví (cloud forest above the city): 45 minutes, $2 return.

## Costs

Colombia offers excellent mid-range value. Budget: $30–50/day. Mid-range: $60–100/day. Luxury: $200–400/day.

- Bandeja paisa (the national dish — rice, beans, chicharrón, egg, arepa): COP 15,000–25,000 ($3.75–6.25) at a local corrientazo (lunch restaurant)
- Coffee (tinto, small black): COP 1,000–2,000 ($0.25–0.50)
- Street empanadas: COP 1,500–3,000 ($0.38–0.75) each
- Mid-range restaurant Medellín: COP 40,000–80,000 ($10–20) per person
- Hostel (private room): $20–40
- Boutique hotel Cartagena walled city: $80–200/night

## Top Experiences

**Medellín — Urban innovation:** Start at Parque Botero (73 oversized Botero sculptures, free). Take the cable car (metrocable) to Santo Domingo Savio for views over the comunas and the famous "urban escalators" of Las Comunas (cable-car to the informal neighbourhood that was once the city's most violent — now a model of urban renewal). The Museo de Arte Moderno (MAMM) and the neighbourhood of Laureles are the city's creative heart.

**Cartagena — Walled City (Ciudad Amurallada):** The 13 km of 16th-century walls and bastions are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Walk the walls at sunset for Caribbean views. The Rosario Islands (1 hour by speedboat, $25–40 return) have excellent snorkelling. The neighbourhood of Getsemaní — once Cartagena's poorest neighbourhood — now has the best street art and nightlife.

**Coffee Region (Eje Cafetero):** Salento is the base. The Cocora Valley (Los Nevados NP) has wax palm trees reaching 60m — the tallest monocots on earth. Day hike: 6–8 km circuit, $8 park entry. Hacienda Venecia (near Manizales) offers the best coffee estate tour: $15, includes cupping. Stay at a finca (coffee farm guesthouse): $40–80/night including meals.

**Tayrona National Park:** The park combines jungle and Caribbean coast. The 3-hour hike from the Zaino entrance to Cabo San Juan camp (the main beach, hammock rental $15–20/night) passes jungle streams and howler monkeys. Snorkelling at El Cabo: coral reefs, parrotfish, and the occasional hammerhead shark 100m offshore.

## Safety

Colombia's security situation has transformed dramatically. Medellín, Cartagena, Bogotá's tourist zones, and the Coffee Region are safe for tourists applying normal urban caution. Flash kidnappings and phone snatching occur in poorly-lit streets and during late-night taxi rides in Bogotá. Use Uber/InDriver rather than hailing street taxis. Avoid border regions with Venezuela and Panama.

## Practical Tips

- Colombian Peso (COP): 1 USD ≈ 4,000 COP. ATMs are available everywhere; use Bancolombia or Davivienda for best rates.
- Tipping: 10% "propina voluntaria" appears on restaurant bills; it is truly voluntary but appreciated.
- Altitude: Bogotá at 2,600m may cause mild altitude symptoms — rest on arrival, stay hydrated.
- Coffee: don't order "café de Colombia" in Colombia — it's the export blend. Ask for "café especial" for the good stuff. Juan Valdez is the Starbucks equivalent; local micro-roasters are infinitely better.
