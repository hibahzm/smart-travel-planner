# Switzerland (Swiss Alps)

## Overview
Switzerland punches above its weight in almost every category: the Alps provide some of Europe's most dramatic landscapes, the train network is the world's most punctual and comprehensive, the food and hotel standards are impeccable, and the cultural heritage — across four language regions — is genuinely fascinating. It is expensive by any measure, but the quality, reliability, and natural beauty justify the premium for those who can afford it.

## Getting There
Switzerland's main international airports are Zürich (ZRH), Geneva (GVA), and Basel (BSL, shared with France and Germany). Zürich is the largest hub with the widest international connections. Most major European cities are within a 2-hour flight. Geneva is the entry point for western Switzerland and the Mont Blanc/Chamonix area. The Swiss Rail network connects airports to city centres in under 15 minutes.

## Getting Around
Switzerland's transport network is a world benchmark:
- **Swiss Federal Railways (SBB)** — trains run on time (97%+ punctuality); clean, comfortable, and frequent (main routes every 30 minutes).
- **Swiss Travel Pass** — unlimited travel on all trains, buses, boats, and most mountain railways; 3/4/6/8/15-day options; a near-essential purchase for visitors spending 3+ days.
- **PostBus** — the iconic yellow buses serving mountain villages not reached by rail; often spectacular scenic routes.
- **Mountain railways, cable cars, and gondolas** — the backbone of Alpine resort access; the Bernina Express, Glacier Express, and Golden Pass are scenic highlights.
- **Driving** — possible but not necessary; requires a Motorway Vignette (CHF 40/year); note that some Alpine roads are closed in winter.

## Regions and Highlights

### Bernese Oberland
The quintessential Swiss Alps; Interlaken is the adventure sports capital and gateway.
- **Jungfraujoch ("Top of Europe")** — 3,454 m; the highest railway station in Europe; glacier panorama and the Aletsch Glacier (UNESCO World Heritage). The cogwheel train from Interlaken takes 2h30 and costs CHF 215 return.
- **Schilthorn** — 2,970 m; reached by cable car from Mürren; the Piz Gloria revolving restaurant was the James Bond film location (On Her Majesty's Secret Service).
- **Lauterbrunnen Valley** — waterfall-filled valley below the Eiger; the Staubbach Falls plunge 297 m. Wengen and Mürren are car-free villages accessible only by train and cable car.
- **Grindelwald** — base for the Eiger North Face; excellent hiking and ski resort.

### Zermatt and the Matterhorn
- **Zermatt** — car-free mountain village at 1,620 m; dominated by the 4,478-m Matterhorn. Electric vehicles only.
- **Gornergrat Bahn** — cogwheel railway to 3,089 m; panoramic view of the Matterhorn and Monte Rosa massif with 29 four-thousanders visible.
- **Zermatt skiing** — ski area extends into Italy (Cervinia); 360 km of runs; year-round skiing on the Klein Matterhorn glacier.

### Graubünden
Switzerland's largest and most wild canton; Romansh is the fourth national language.
- **Davos and Klosters** — Alpine sports resorts; Davos hosts the World Economic Forum.
- **Engadine Valley** — St Moritz (glamorous winter resort), Silvaplana (kitesurfing on Lake Silvaplana), and the Swiss National Park.
- **Bernina Pass and Bernina Express** — UNESCO World Heritage railway crossing Europe's highest railway pass at 2,253 m; runs Chur–Tirano (Italy); dramatic viaducts and glaciers.

### Central Switzerland — Lucerne
- **Lucerne (Luzern)** — the most picturesque Swiss city; the medieval Chapel Bridge (Kapellbrücke, 1365), the roaring Lion Monument, and a backdrop of snow-capped mountains.
- **Lake Lucerne** — boat trips to Tellskapelle (Tell legend site) and the summit of Mount Rigi and Pilatus.
- **Mount Pilatus** — 2,132 m; the world's steepest cogwheel railway (48% gradient) reaches the summit; panoramic Alpine views.

### Geneva and Lake Geneva
- **Geneva** — international city; United Nations European HQ, CERN, Red Cross Museum; Lake Geneva and the Jet d'Eau fountain (140 m high).
- **Lausanne** — Olympic capital; the Olympic Museum and cathedral are highlights.
- **Lake Geneva wine region** — Lavaux terraced vineyards (UNESCO) producing excellent Chasselas white wine; the golden afternoon light is extraordinary.
- **Montreux** — home of the Jazz Festival (July); Château de Chillon (medieval island castle) is Switzerland's most visited historical monument.

## Activities
- **Skiing and snowboarding** — December–April; world-class resorts in Verbier (freeride), Zermatt (most scenic), Davos-Klosters (largest area), and Engelberg.
- **Hiking** — Switzerland has 65,000 km of marked trails; the Via Alpina (long-distance route), the Haute Route (Chamonix to Zermatt), and the Appenzell Innerrhoden trails are highlights.
- **Via Ferrata** — iron-clad climbing routes for all levels; excellent near Kandersteg and the Eiger.
- **Mountain biking** — Verbier, Flims-Laax, and Nendaz have world-class biking infrastructure.
- **Paragliding** — from Interlaken, Lucerne, and Ticino; tandem flights from CHF 170.
- **Swimming in alpine lakes** — Lake Brienz (impossibly turquoise glacial water), Lake Lugano, and Lake Lucerne are all popular in summer.

## Eat and Drink
Swiss cuisine varies by region:
- **Fondue** — melted Gruyère and Emmental cheese, eaten in bread cubes; the winter social ritual; authentic in any mountain restaurant.
- **Raclette** — melted cheese scraped over potatoes, cornichons, and pickled onions; simpler and more satisfying than it sounds.
- **Rösti** — crispy fried potato cake from Bern; often served with fried eggs and bacon.
- **Zürcher Geschnetzeltes** — veal strips in cream mushroom sauce; the signature dish of Zürich.
- **Chocolate** — Lindt, Toblerone, and Läderach (fresh chocolate) are world-famous; factory visits near Zürich and Broc.
- **Wine** — Swiss wine is almost never exported (80% consumed domestically); Chasselas (Vaud), Pinot Noir (Graubünden), and Merlot (Ticino) are the principal varieties.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Swiss Franc (CHF); 1 CHF ≈ USD 1.12. Switzerland is one of the world's most expensive countries.
- **Languages**: German (65%), French (23%), Italian (8%), Romansh (1%).
- **Safety**: Extremely safe; one of the lowest crime rates in Europe.
- **Best time to visit**: December–March for skiing; June–September for hiking (cable cars and mountain railways are fully operational). July–August is peak season with the best weather.
- **Budget**: High; mid-range CHF 200–350/day (USD 220–390). A mountain railway day excursion costs CHF 60–200. Budget by Europe's standards is still expensive.
- **Tipping**: Service is included in restaurant bills; rounding up by CHF 1–2 is customary but not obligatory.
