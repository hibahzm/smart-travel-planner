# Maldives

## Overview
The Maldives is an archipelago nation of 1,192 coral islands grouped into 26 natural atolls in the Indian Ocean, straddling the equator south-southwest of India. With an average ground elevation of 1.5 m above sea level — the world's lowest — the Maldives is both extraordinarily beautiful and acutely vulnerable to climate change. It is synonymous with overwater bungalows, gin-clear turquoise lagoons, and some of the world's finest marine biodiversity.

## Getting There
Velana International Airport (MLE) on Malé is the gateway. Most visitors arrive on flights from Dubai (4h), Singapore (3h45), or Colombo (1h). From Malé, transfers to resorts or guesthouses are by seaplane (15–45 minutes, USD 300–600 return, limited to daytime operation), speedboat (30 minutes to 3 hours), or domestic flight followed by speedboat. All resorts handle their own transfers — book through the resort.

## Getting Around
The Maldives is not a typical destination for independent island-hopping. There are two distinct travel models:

**Resort islands** — single-resort private islands, or islands shared by 2–3 resorts. All-inclusive or full-board pricing. Guests rarely leave the island. Water villa living, house reef snorkelling, and spa treatments define the experience. Most transfers are by seaplane or speedboat arranged by the resort.

**Local islands** — inhabited islands where independent travellers can stay in guesthouses from USD 50–120/night. The largest guesthouse hubs are Maafushi (South Malé Atoll), Dhigurah (South Ari Atoll), and Fulidhoo. Ferries connect islands; schedules are limited but improving. Bikinis are not permitted on inhabited islands (bikini beaches are designated; resorts are exempt).

## Atolls and Regions
**North Malé Atoll** — closest to the airport; Vabbinfaru, Baros, and Soneva Jani are notable resorts. Good for budget-conscious travellers on local islands (Maafushi, Guraidhoo).
**South Malé Atoll** — Maafushi is the most popular local island; excellent diving on the Kaafu Atoll channels.
**Ari Atoll** — famous for whale shark aggregations (year-round), manta rays, and some of the Maldives' most prestigious resorts (Four Seasons Landaa Giraavaru, W Maldives, Conrad Rangali).
**Baa Atoll (UNESCO Biosphere Reserve)** — Hanifaru Bay is the world's largest known manta ray feeding aggregation site; accessible June–November.
**Lhaviyani Atoll** — Kuredu and Komandoo; beautiful reefs; generally less expensive than southern atolls.
**Addu Atoll (Seenu)** — southernmost atoll; accessible by domestic flight; the only atoll with road connections between islands; home to diving sites with WWII wrecks.

## See and Do
- **Snorkelling on house reefs** — most resort and local islands have reef accessible directly from the beach or jetty; nurse sharks, reef sharks, and sea turtles are common.
- **Diving** — the Maldives has over 2,000 dive sites. Top: Maaya Thila (Ari Atoll), Fish Head (Ari), the Kuredu Channel (Lhaviyani), and Lankan Falls (North Malé).
- **Whale shark watching** — South Ari Atoll has a resident population; year-round encounters, best June–November.
- **Manta ray diving** — Hanifaru Bay in Baa Atoll from June–November; also Bathalaa Maaga Kandu in South Malé year-round.
- **Dolphin cruises** — sunset dhoni (traditional boat) cruises to spot spinner dolphins; virtually every resort and guesthouse offers this.
- **Sandbanks** — exposed sandbars appear and disappear with tides and seasons; resorts arrange picnic trips.
- **Surfing** — the Maldives has world-class surf breaks, mostly in the Malé atolls; Chickens, Cokes, and Sultans are legendary right-hand reef breaks. Best: February–October.
- **Overwater villas** — the iconic experience; Soneva Jani, Gili Lankanfushi, and Four Seasons Landaa Giraavaru set the standard.

## Marine Life
The Maldives is one of the world's top dive destinations:
- 1,100+ species of fish including whale sharks, tiger sharks, hammerheads (Fuvahmulah), and over 20 species of shark.
- 5 species of sea turtle; all species are commonly encountered on reefs.
- Manta rays at Hanifaru Bay in numbers up to 200 in a single feeding event.
- Dolphins: spinner dolphins, common dolphins, and occasionally blue and sperm whales offshore.
- Coral cover is recovering from mass bleaching events (1998, 2016); healthier corals exist on deeper, cooler reefs.

## Eat and Drink
Food is primarily served within resorts, where full-board (or all-inclusive) pricing is standard:
- **Mas huni** — tuna, coconut, and onion mixed and eaten with roshi flatbread; Maldivian breakfast staple.
- **Garudhiya** — fish broth served with rice, lime, chilli, and onion; the national soup.
- **Fihunu mas** — grilled fish with coconut chutney; common across local islands.
- Resort restaurants range from casual beachside grills to fine-dining underwater restaurants (Ithaa at Conrad Rangali, 5 m below the lagoon surface).
- Alcohol is only served on resort islands and on the "inhabited island" designated tourist beaches/bars; guesthouses on local islands are dry.

## Sleep
**Ultra-luxury**: Soneva Jani, Gili Lankanfushi, Cheval Blanc Randheli, North Island (USD 2,000–8,000+/night).
**Luxury**: Four Seasons, Conrad, W Maldives, Niyama (USD 700–2,000/night).
**Mid-range resorts**: Kanuhura, Cinnamon Dhonveli, OBLU Select Lobigili (USD 300–700/night).
**Guesthouses** (local islands): USD 50–120/night; best in Maafushi and Dhigurah.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Maldivian Rufiyaa (MVR); USD is accepted virtually everywhere.
- **Language**: Dhivehi; English spoken in all tourist contexts.
- **Safety**: Extremely safe; crime against tourists is rare. The greatest risks are ocean-related (strong currents, sunburn).
- **Best time to visit**: November–April (northeast monsoon season) for calm, sunny weather with excellent visibility. May–October (southwest monsoon) is wetter with stronger winds; surfing is better.
- **Budget**: Highly variable; USD 80–150/day on local islands; USD 500–2,000+/day in resorts. There is no truly budget resort experience.
- **Environment**: The Maldives is acutely affected by rising sea levels and coral bleaching. Choose resorts with strong sustainability credentials (Soneva, Six Senses).
