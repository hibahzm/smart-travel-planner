# Barcelona, Spain

## Overview
Barcelona, capital of Catalonia, is arguably Europe's most seductive city — a place that seamlessly combines world-class architecture, excellent beaches, a legendary food scene, and a nightlife that begins when most cities are going to bed. Gaudí's surrealist masterpieces dot the city, the Gothic Quarter hides 2,000 years of history in its labyrinthine lanes, and La Barceloneta beach is a 10-minute walk from the opera house. Barcelona rewards every type of traveller.

## Getting There
El Prat Airport (BCN) is 12 km southwest of the city. The Aerobus takes 35 minutes to Plaça de Catalunya (€5.90 one-way, €10.20 return). The RENFE R2 Nord train is slightly slower (38 minutes) but cheaper (€4.60). Taxis cost approximately €30–40. Barcelona is also a major cruise port and Eurobus hub; high-speed AVE trains connect to Madrid (2h30) and Paris (6h30 via TGV).

## Getting Around
**Metro** — the TMB metro network has 12 lines and covers the whole city; single trips €2.40, T-Casual 10-trip card €11.35. The T-Casual is the best value for most visitors.
**Bus** — extensive network; particularly useful for reaching Parc Güell, the hills, and beaches.
**Cycling** — Bicing bike-share is for residents; tourist rental bikes are available at dozens of shops. The seafront and Passeig de Gràcia are great cycling routes. Beware traffic on narrow Gothic Quarter streets.
**Walking** — central Barcelona is very walkable; the Rambla, Gothic Quarter, and Barceloneta are all on foot from Plaça de Catalunya.
**Cable car** — Teleférico del Puerto connects Barceloneta beach to Montjuïc.

## Neighbourhoods
**Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic)** — ancient Roman colony (2,000-year-old walls visible), medieval cathedral, narrow alleys; atmospheric but crowded with tourists.
**El Born (Sant Pere)** — the Picasso Museum, Santa Maria del Mar church, and Barcelona's most stylish bar and restaurant scene.
**Barceloneta** — the fishing neighbourhood turned beach district; seafood restaurants, chiringuitos (beach bars), and a 1.5 km sandy beach.
**Eixample** — the 19th-century grid expansion; Gaudí's Sagrada Família and Casa Batlló; upscale dining and shopping on Passeig de Gràcia.
**Gràcia** — bohemian, local, hilly; Plaça del Sol is the neighbourhood heart; Mercado de l'Abaceria; excellent for escaping tourist crowds.
**Poblenou** — former industrial district; now a creative hub with design studios, concept stores, and the best coffee in Barcelona.
**Montjuïc** — hilltop park with Olympic Stadium, MNAC museum, Fundació Joan Miró, and spectacular city views.

## See — Gaudí's Barcelona
Antoni Gaudí's organic, surrealist architecture defines Barcelona's visual identity:
- **Sagrada Família** — Gaudí's masterpiece; under continuous construction since 1882; the Nativity Façade (Gaudí's original) and the nave's forest of branching columns are transcendent. Book tickets months in advance; tower access requires separate booking.
- **Park Güell** — terraced gardens, the mosaic salamander, the famous bench with city views, and Gaudí's former house. The central area (Monumental Zone) requires ticketed entry.
- **Casa Batlló** — Passeig de Gràcia; "House of Bones"; extraordinary dragon-spine roof and coloured ceramic façade. Rooftop and "magic nights" are the highlights.
- **Casa Milà (La Pedrera)** — undulating limestone building; the rooftop with its warrior chimneys is unmissable; interior apartment recreated as 1910s domestic space.
- **Palau Güell** — early Gaudí; more restrained than his later work; UNESCO listed; central Raval.
- **Colònia Güell (Crypt)** — 30 minutes from Barcelona; the structural experiment that led to the Sagrada Família; rarely visited and architecturally fascinating.

## See — Other
- **Picasso Museum** — 4,251 works tracing Picasso's development from childhood to Cubist mastery; book ahead.
- **MNAC (National Art Museum of Catalonia)** — Romanesque and Gothic Catalan art; the building itself (Palau Nacional on Montjuïc) is spectacular.
- **Fundació Joan Miró** — major retrospective of the Surrealist master; excellent temporary exhibitions.
- **Palau de la Música Catalana** — Domènech i Montaner's Art Nouveau concert hall; UNESCO listed; guided tours or evening concerts.
- **Mercat de la Boqueria** — famous covered market on Las Ramblas; sadly now mostly tourist food stalls. The authentic markets are Sant Antoni (Eixample) and Santa Caterina (El Born).

## Eat and Drink
Barcelona's food scene is extraordinary at every level:
- **Tapas and pintxos** — La Cova Fumada (inventors of the bombeta, the battered potato ball), El Xampanyet in El Born, and Bar Cañete are institutions.
- **Seafood** — fresh from the Mediterranean; suquet de peix (fish stew), esqueixada (salt cod salad), and fideuà (noodle paella) are Catalan seafood classics.
- **Vermouth (vermut)** — the Sunday pre-lunch ritual; El Xampanyet and Bar Calders serve the best.
- **Fine dining** — Disfrutar (run by former El Bulli chefs; 3 Michelin stars and frequently ranked world's best restaurant), Tickets (Albert Adrià's tapas bar), and Alkimia represent Barcelona at its highest level.
- **Wine** — Catalan wines (Priorat, Penedès) are excellent and underpriced; ask for house wine.
- **Nightlife** — clubs don't fill until 2 am; Pacha, Razzmatazz, and Sala Apolo run until 6 am or later. The Barceloneta seafront has year-round beach club culture.

## Sleep
Luxury: Hotel Arts Barcelona (Ritz-Carlton) on Barceloneta beach; Mandarin Oriental on Passeig de Gràcia; W Barcelona (the sail-shaped hotel on the beach). Mid-range: boutique hotels in El Born and Eixample from €140–250/night. Budget: hostels in the Gothic Quarter from €25–45/night in dorms; apartments on Airbnb offer better value than hotels for groups.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR).
- **Language**: Catalan and Spanish; English widely spoken in tourist areas.
- **Safety**: Generally safe; pickpocketing is extremely common on Las Ramblas, in the Gothic Quarter, and on the Metro. Never put your phone on a restaurant table.
- **Best time to visit**: May–June and September–October for warm weather (22–28°C) without July–August peak crowds. August is the hottest and most expensive month.
- **Budget**: Mid-range €120–200/day including accommodation, meals, and Gaudí entry fees (€25–40 per attraction).
- **Local tip**: Residents of Barcelona know the tourist traps and avoid them; La Rambla seafood restaurants and hotel-adjacent bars are generally overpriced.
