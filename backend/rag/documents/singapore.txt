# Singapore

## Overview
Singapore is one of the world's smallest countries and one of its most remarkable. A city-state of just 733 km² with a population of 5.9 million drawn from Chinese, Malay, Indian, and dozens of other ethnicities, Singapore is simultaneously the world's most efficient transport hub, a food paradise, a tropical garden city, and a showcase for ultra-modern urban planning. It is the safest, cleanest, and arguably most liveable city in Southeast Asia.

## Getting There
Changi Airport (SIN) is consistently rated the world's best airport. Its four main terminals (Terminal 5 under construction) and the Jewel shopping complex (complete with a 40-m indoor waterfall) make arrival an event in itself. The MRT East-West Line connects Changi to the city centre in 30 minutes (SGD 1.80–2.30). Taxis are metered and readily available; Grab is cheaper for most rides.

## Getting Around
Singapore's MRT (Mass Rapid Transit) network is extensive, clean, air-conditioned, and runs from 5:30 am to midnight. EZ-Link cards or the Singapore Tourist Pass give unlimited travel. Buses cover every corner. Taxis are metered and honest; Grab is the dominant ride-hailing app. Walking is comfortable in air-conditioned malls and underground connections but the equatorial heat and humidity make outdoor walking strenuous — plan around 10-minute walks, not 30-minute ones.

## Neighbourhoods
**Marina Bay** — the gleaming financial centre; Marina Bay Sands, the Merlion, ArtScience Museum, and the Gardens by the Bay.
**Chinatown** — dense, atmospheric; Sri Mariamman Hindu temple, hawker centres, traditional medicine shops, and the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple.
**Little India (Tekka)** — around Serangoon Road; Mustafa Centre (a 24-hour department store that is a Singapore institution), spice markets, flower garlands, and the Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple.
**Kampong Gelam** — Malay heritage quarter; Sultan Mosque, Bussorah Street, and the hip Arab Street with boutique shops and cafes.
**Orchard Road** — the main shopping corridor; ION Orchard, Ngee Ann City, and dozens of malls.
**Holland Village and Dempsey Hill** — expat-heavy neighbourhoods with excellent restaurants and bars.
**Tiong Bahru** — pre-war art deco housing estate now colonised by independent bookshops, brunch cafes, and artisan bakeries; Singapore's Brooklyn.
**Sentosa Island** — Universal Studios, beach clubs, casino, and the world-famous ONE°15 Marina.

## See
- **Gardens by the Bay** — 250 ha of futuristic gardens; the Supertree Grove is especially spectacular at night during the light-and-music show (free from outside). Cloud Forest and Flower Dome conservatories charge entry.
- **Marina Bay Sands SkyPark** — 57th-floor observation deck and infinity pool (the pool is hotel guests only); views take in the entire city skyline and southern Malaysian peninsula.
- **Singapore Zoo and Night Safari** — the Night Safari pioneered nocturnal wildlife tourism; the Singapore Zoo is a global benchmark for ethical, habitat-style animal viewing.
- **Botanic Gardens** — UNESCO World Heritage listed; 160 years old; the National Orchid Garden houses 1,000+ orchid species.
- **National Museum of Singapore** — Singapore's history from pre-colonial times to independence; free entry to permanent galleries.
- **Chinatown Heritage Centre** — reconstructed Chinatown shophouse apartments documenting the lives of early Chinese immigrants.
- **ArtScience Museum** — lotus-shaped museum at Marina Bay Sands; excellent temporary exhibitions on art-technology intersections.
- **Haw Par Villa** — bizarre and fascinating 1930s theme park of Chinese mythology with thousands of dioramas; entry free.

## Do
- Eat your way through a hawker centre — Maxwell Food Centre, Newton Food Centre, and Chinatown Complex are the most famous. Singapore's hawker culture is UNESCO-recognised.
- Visit Universal Studios Singapore on Sentosa.
- Walk the Southern Ridges — a series of elevated forest trails connecting Mount Faber to Kent Ridge; free, beautiful, and almost unknown.
- Take the cable car from Mount Faber to Sentosa for harbour views.
- Shop duty-free at Changi Airport — consistently the world's best airport retail.
- Catch a performance at the Esplanade — Theatres on the Bay (the "durian" building).

## Eat and Drink
Singapore is one of the world's great food cities, built on the intersection of Chinese, Malay, Indian, Peranakan, and Western cuisines:
- **Chicken rice (Hainanese)** — the unofficial national dish; poached or roasted chicken with fragrant rice cooked in chicken stock and ginger.
- **Laksa** — coconut curry noodle soup; Katong laksa (spoon-cut noodles) is the Singapore variant.
- **Chilli crab** — iconic Singapore seafood; sweet-spicy tomato-based sauce, best at Jumbo Seafood or Long Beach.
- **Roti prata** — flaky flatbread eaten with curry; Indian-Muslim breakfast staple; excellent at Springleaf Prata Place.
- **Char kway teow** — flat rice noodles wok-fried with egg, beansprouts, lard, and chilli.
- **Teh tarik** — "pulled tea"; sweetened black tea with condensed milk, poured theatrically between cups to create a frothy head.
- **Hawker centres** — communal food courts where individual stalls serve single dishes mastered over generations; meals cost SGD 3–8. Liao Fan Hong Kong Soya Sauce Chicken Rice (Chinatown) is Michelin-starred and costs SGD 3.

## Sleep
Singapore is an expensive city for accommodation. Luxury: Marina Bay Sands, the Raffles Hotel (recently restored to its colonial glory), the Capella on Sentosa. Mid-range: boutique hotels in Tiong Bahru and Kampong Gelam for SGD 150–280/night. Budget: well-run hostels in Chinatown and Little India from SGD 25–50/night.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Singapore Dollar (SGD); 1 USD ≈ SGD 1.34.
- **Language**: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil all official; English is the language of business and daily life.
- **Safety**: Among the world's safest cities; violent crime is virtually non-existent. Drug trafficking carries the death penalty; laws are strictly enforced.
- **Best time to visit**: Year-round equatorial climate (26–32°C); February and June are slightly drier. Rain falls in short, intense showers year-round.
- **Budget**: Expensive by Southeast Asian standards; budget USD 100–150/day, mid-range USD 180–300/day.
- **Laws**: No chewing gum sold; jaywalking fines; littering fines. Singapore's laws are strict and enforced — respect them.
