## Iceland — Destination Guide

Iceland is the world's northernmost capital-city country, a volcanic island of 330,000 people perched on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. It offers superlatives that exist nowhere else in Europe: active volcanoes, midnight sun, northern lights, geysers erupting every 5–8 minutes, and thousands of kilometres of unpaved F-roads through lava fields and glaciers.

## Overview

Iceland divides naturally into the Ring Road (Route 1, 1,339 km paved loop around the island), the Golden Circle (the most-visited day-trip from Reykjavik), the South Coast, the Westfjords (remote, spectacular, minimal tourist infrastructure), and the interior (F-roads: 4WD mandatory, summer only).

## Best Time to Visit

**June–August:** Midnight sun. 24-hour daylight in June. Temperatures 10–15°C in Reykjavik, warmer inland. All F-roads open. The Westfjords are accessible. Puffin colonies active (cliffs near Vik, Látrabjarg in Westfjords). Peak season — book everything months ahead.

**September–October:** Northern lights begin (requires darkness, so from mid-August). Fewer crowds, lower prices, dramatic autumn colours on the moorland. Some highland F-roads close in October.

**November–March:** Northern lights season peak. The aurora is active but requires clear skies and dark nights — check forecast apps (Veður.is). Temperatures −5 to 5°C in the south; much colder in the interior. Many waterfalls and roads freeze; Vatnajökull tours continue year-round. The Blue Lagoon operates year-round (must book weeks ahead online: ~$60–100).

## Getting In

Keflavik Airport (KEF) serves all international flights. Flybus or bus/taxi to Reykjavik: 45 minutes, ~$25. Car rental is mandatory for exploring beyond Reykjavik. Book online months in advance; peak season rental prices double or triple.

## Getting Around

A car is essential. A standard 2WD covers the Ring Road and Golden Circle. For F-roads (Landmannalaugar, Askja, Þórsmörk), you need a 4WD — a legal and insurance requirement. Do not take a 2WD on F-roads; rivers must be forded and the terrain is destructive. Rental: from $60/day (2WD compact) to $200/day (large 4WD) in summer. Fuel costs ~$2.50/litre; full tank every 200 km on average.

## Costs

Iceland is one of Europe's most expensive countries. Budget: $120–150/day. Mid-range: $200–300. Careful management: $80–100/day if camping and self-catering.

- Guesthouse outside Reykjavik: $80–150/night
- Camping (officially designated sites): $15–20/person/night (required — wild camping banned since 2015)
- Restaurant meal in Reykjavik: $25–50
- Grocery store (Bonus supermarket): self-catering saves 60–70% vs restaurants
- Northern lights tour: $80–120
- Whale watching: $80–100
- Blue Lagoon entry: $65–100

## Top Experiences

**Golden Circle (1 day):** Þingvellir National Park (walk between tectonic plates, the site of the world's oldest parliament), Geysir (Strokkur erupts every 5–8 minutes), Gullfoss waterfall. Easily self-driven; bus tours available from Reykjavik.

**South Coast (1–2 days):** Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss waterfalls (walk behind Seljalandsfoss), the black sand beach at Reynisfjara (dangerous sneaker waves — do not approach the waves), the ice lagoon Jökulsárlón and Diamond Beach (glacial icebergs wash ashore). Vatnajökull glacier hike: $80–120.

**Snæfellsnes Peninsula:** Jules Verne's gateway to the earth's centre. Snæfellsjökull glacier volcano, seal colonies, Kirkjufell mountain (the "arrowhead mountain" from Game of Thrones). 3-hour drive from Reykjavik.

**Westfjords:** Iceland's least-visited region. Dramatic fjord scenery, Látrabjarg bird cliffs (largest in Europe), Dynjandi waterfall. 8-hour drive from Reykjavik. Requires commitment; roads are rough.

**Northern Lights:** Unpredictable — check Veður.is cloud cover and activity forecasts nightly. Drive 30 minutes from Reykjavik (Þingvellir or Ölkelduháls hill) to escape light pollution. Best months October–March.

## Volcanic Activity

Iceland has an active volcanic system. The Reykjanes Peninsula (near Keflavik airport) has seen eruptions since 2021 with new fissures opening periodically. The eruption areas are often viewable from safe distances — follow Veður.is official guidance. Never approach active lava flows without professional guidance.

## Practical Tips

- Insurance: car rental insurance is mandatory; gravel/sand damage coverage (SAAP) is strongly recommended as Iceland's unpaved roads regularly stone-chip windscreens.
- The midnight sun disorients sleep schedules — bring a sleep mask.
- Swimwear is essential: Iceland's geothermal hot pots (including free public ones at Seljavallalaug and Landmannalaugar) are a core part of the experience.
- Leave no trace strictly enforced. Iceland's moss is 50–100 years old and cannot recover from footprints. Stay on marked paths.
