# Costa Rica

## Overview
Costa Rica punches far above its weight for a country the size of West Virginia. It protects over 25% of its land in national parks and reserves, hosts 5% of the world's biodiversity despite covering only 0.03% of its surface area, and pioneered sustainable tourism decades before it became fashionable. For wildlife, adventure sports, and ecological wonder, Costa Rica is unmatched in the Americas.

## Getting There
Juan Santamaría International Airport (SJO) in San José handles most international arrivals. Daniel Oduber International Airport (LIR) in Liberia serves the Guanacaste Pacific coast and is closer to beach destinations — if your itinerary focuses on the northwest, fly into Liberia. Domestic flights (SANSA, Green Airways) connect San José to key destinations like Tortuguero, Quepos (for Manuel Antonio), and Drake Bay in under an hour.

## Getting Around
**Shuttle services** — shared and private shuttles run by Interbus, Grayline, and Monkey Ride are the most popular way to travel between tourist towns; comfortable, punctual, and reasonably priced (USD 45–90 between major destinations).
**Public buses** — run from San José's main terminals to virtually everywhere; cheap (USD 5–12) but slow and sometimes unreliable.
**4WD rental** — essential if you want maximum flexibility, especially for the Osa Peninsula, Monteverde, and rural dirt roads. Mandatory CDW insurance adds USD 15–30/day to rental costs.
**Domestic flights** — worth considering for reaching the remote Osa Peninsula, Tortuguero, or when time is short.

## Regions and Highlights
**Arenal / La Fortuna** — the cone-shaped Arenal Volcano presides over a region of hot springs, rainforest, hanging bridges, and adventure sports. Best hot springs: Baldi, La Fortuna, and the free Rio Chollin natural thermal river.

**Monteverde** — cloud forests at 1,500 m altitude; the Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve protects 10,500 ha of primary forest. Night walks reveal tarantulas, glass frogs, and kinkajous. Excellent canopy zip-lining at Sky Adventures and Selvatura.

**Manuel Antonio** — small national park with extraordinary wildlife density; sloths, squirrel monkeys, and white-faced capuchins are reliably seen along the beach trails. Combine with the resort town of Quepos.

**Osa Peninsula (Corcovado)** — the most biodiverse region; Corcovado National Park is accessible only by air or multi-hour boat/hike. Jaguar sightings are possible but rare; tapirs, harpy eagles, and scarlet macaws are regular.

**Tortuguero** — Caribbean coast accessible only by boat or plane; nesting sea turtles (July–October) and river wildlife watching.

**Tamarindo / Guanacaste** — Pacific beach towns with consistent surf breaks and dry-season sunshine (December–April); more developed and touristy but reliable beach weather.

**Caribbean Coast (Puerto Viejo)** — Afro-Caribbean culture, punta rhythms, Bribri indigenous communities, and Cahuita National Park's coral reef snorkelling; significantly more rainfall than the Pacific.

## Wildlife
Costa Rica's biodiversity is staggering for its size:
- **Birds**: 900+ species including resplendent quetzal, scarlet macaw, and 50 species of hummingbird.
- **Mammals**: tapir, jaguar, ocelot, 4 species of monkey, giant anteater, 6 species of wild cat.
- **Reptiles**: American crocodiles (highly visible at the Tárcoles River bridge), Leatherback sea turtles.
- **Amphibians**: 200+ frog species including the iconic red-eyed tree frog and the poison-dart frogs of the Osa.
- Best wildlife watching: Corcovado, Tortuguero, the Monteverde bridges, and any canal waterway.

## Adventures
- **White-water rafting**: Río Pacuare (considered one of the world's best rivers, Class III–IV) and Reventazón.
- **Zip-lining**: World-class canopy tours in Monteverde, Arenal, and Manuel Antonio.
- **Surfing**: Tamarindo, Nosara, Pavones (world's longest left break), and Dominical for all skill levels.
- **Hiking**: Corcovado (ranger-guide required), Arenal Volcano trails, and Chirripó summit (Costa Rica's highest peak at 3,821 m).
- **Sea kayaking and snorkelling**: Cahuita, Ballena Marine National Park, and around the Osa Peninsula.

## Eat and Drink
Costa Rican cuisine is simple and hearty:
- **Gallo pinto** — rice and black beans, the national breakfast; eaten morning, noon, and night.
- **Casado** — a plate of rice, beans, salad, protein (chicken, fish, or beef), and fried plantains; USD 5–10 in a soda (family restaurant).
- **Ceviche** — fresh fish marinated in lime and served with patacones (fried plantain chips); excellent on the coasts.
- **Chifrijo** — rice, beans, chicharrones (pork crackling), and pico de gallo; bar snack staple.
- **Coffee** — Costa Rica grows some of the world's finest arabica; a cup from Tarrazú, Naranjo, or Tres Ríos is obligatory.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Costa Rican Colón (CRC); USD widely accepted, especially in tourist areas.
- **Language**: Spanish; English widely spoken in tourist regions.
- **Safety**: Safe by regional standards; petty theft in San José; use hotel safes and avoid displaying valuables.
- **Best time to visit**: December–April (dry season, Pacific coast). July–August has a brief dry window (veranillo). Osa Peninsula is accessible year-round but July–November is very wet.
- **Budget**: Moderate; USD 70–120/day mid-range. Park entrance fees (USD 15–18/day) and shuttles add up quickly.
- **Eco-tips**: Stay in certified sustainable tourism (CST) lodges, hire local guides, and obey wildlife-viewing rules — do not feed animals.
