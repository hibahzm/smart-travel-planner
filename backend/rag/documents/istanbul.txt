# Istanbul, Turkey

## Overview
Istanbul is the only city in the world that spans two continents, straddling Europe and Asia across the Bosphorus Strait. Once capital of the Byzantine and Ottoman empires, Istanbul wears its 2,500-year history lightly — Blue Mosques and Byzantine mosaics neighbour hip wine bars and contemporary art spaces. The city's food scene is among the world's best, its bazaars are legendary, and its skyline, punctuated by minarets and the Bosphorus, is unforgettable.

## Getting There
Istanbul Airport (IST) on the European side is the city's main hub, one of the world's busiest. The Havaist airport bus (45–90 minutes, TRY 80) and metro (45 minutes, TRY 40) connect to central Istanbul. Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW) on the Asian side serves budget carriers; the Havabus from SAW to Taksim takes 60–90 minutes depending on traffic. High-speed trains connect Istanbul (Halkali) to Edirne; ferry services run to Greek islands seasonally.

## Getting Around
**Metro and Tram**: The T1 tram runs from Kabataş through Eminönü to Bağcılar, stopping at Sultanahmet (tourist heart) and the Grand Bazaar. Metro Line M2 connects Taksim to the airport. The Marmaray commuter rail crosses under the Bosphorus.
**Istanbulkart**: A rechargeable transit card covering all metro, tram, bus, ferry, and funicular; purchase at any station. Transfers within 2 hours are nearly free.
**Ferries**: The most beautiful and practical way to cross the Bosphorus; IDO and Şehir Hatları run frequent ferries between Eminönü/Karaköy and Asian-side districts (Kadıköy, Üsküdar).
**Taxis**: Often try to overcharge tourists; insist on the meter or use BiTaksi / Uber.
**Walking**: Sultanahmet, the Grand Bazaar area, and Beyoğlu/Karaköy are best explored on foot.

## Neighbourhoods
**Sultanahmet** — the old city; Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace, and the Hippodrome; touristy but inescapable.
**Beyoğlu / İstiklal Avenue** — the European heart of modern Istanbul; the pedestrian İstiklal Caddesi stretches 1.4 km, flanked by 19th-century embassies-turned-consulates, churches, restaurants, and shops. The Nostalgic Tram (T2) runs its length.
**Karaköy and Galata** — waterfront neighbourhood with the Galata Tower, hip coffee shops, and the best fish sandwiches in Istanbul.
**Balat and Fener** — historic Jewish and Greek Orthodox neighbourhoods; colourful houses on steep hills; excellent local cafes.
**Kadıköy (Asian side)** — the real Istanbul; young, liberal, foodie; the Kadıköy market and Moda neighbourhood are excellent.
**Beşiktaş and Ortaköy** — Bosphorus-side neighbourhoods with the Çırağan Palace, Dolmabahçe, and the Instagram-famous Ortaköy mosque.

## See
- **Hagia Sophia** — built 537 CE as a Byzantine cathedral, later Ottoman mosque, then museum, and reconverted to a mosque in 2020; the 55-m dome remains breathtaking. Entry is now free for Muslims and tourists visiting outside prayer times.
- **Topkapı Palace** — nerve centre of the Ottoman Empire for 400 years; the Harem, Treasury (displaying the Topkapı Dagger and the Spoonmaker's Diamond), and views over the Bosphorus are highlights. Book in advance for Harem tours.
- **Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque)** — six minarets and 20,000 Iznik tiles; free entry but closed to non-worshippers during prayer times.
- **Grand Bazaar (Kapalıçarşı)** — 4,000+ shops in a covered labyrinth; the world's oldest and largest covered market, operating since 1461.
- **Spice Bazaar (Mısır Çarşısı)** — next to the Grand Bazaar; sells Turkish tea, saffron, dried fruit, and lokum (Turkish delight).
- **Basilica Cistern** — vast 6th-century underground reservoir supported by 336 columns; atmospheric and slightly eerie.
- **Chora Church (Kariye Museum)** — extraordinary 14th-century Byzantine mosaics and frescoes; currently (2024) being reconverted to a mosque but still partially accessible.
- **Dolmabahçe Palace** — 19th-century Ottoman palace on the Bosphorus shore; opulent and somewhat overwrought.

## Do
- Take an early-morning Bosphorus ferry to watch the sunrise over the minarets.
- Visit the Princes' Islands (Büyükada) by ferry — car-free islands with horse-drawn carriages and wooden Victorian houses.
- Shop for hand-painted ceramics, evil eye amulets, and Turkish lamps in the Grand Bazaar backstreets (avoid the main corridors).
- Attend a Whirling Dervishes ceremony at the Sirkeci station or Galata Mevlevihanesi.
- Watch a sunset from Galata Tower, Pierre Loti Hill, or a rooftop bar in Beyoğlu.

## Eat and Drink
Istanbul's food scene is extraordinary and deeply varied:
- **Balık ekmek** — grilled fish sandwich served from boats moored at Galata Bridge; a Istanbul icon (TRY 40–60).
- **Döner kebab** — the genuine article; best found in Beşiktaş and Kadıköy, not Sultanahmet.
- **Meyhane** — Turkish taverns; order a spread of meze (hummus, haydari, stuffed vine leaves, white cheese) and grilled fish with rakı (anise spirit).
- **Simit** — sesame-crusted bread ring sold by street vendors; breakfast staple with tea.
- **Turkish breakfast (kahvaltı)** — a lavish spread of cheeses, olives, tomatoes, eggs, honey, and clotted cream; Sunday brunch at a Bosphorus-view restaurant is a rite of passage.
- **Baklava** — Güllüoğlu in Karaköy is arguably the world's best; eat fresh, not from a display case.
- **Bars** — Beyoğlu's Nevizade Sokak, Karaköy's Kılıç Ali Paşa area, and Kadıköy's Moda bars are the city's best.

## Sleep
Istanbul has excellent options across all budgets. Luxury: the Four Seasons Sultanahmet (in a former Ottoman prison), Çırağan Palace Kempinski, and Six Senses Kocataş Mansions. Mid-range: boutique hotels in Beyoğlu and Galata for TRY 2,000–5,000/night. Budget: hostels in Sultanahmet from TRY 400–700/night.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Turkish Lira (TRY); significant inflation in recent years means prices in USD/EUR are better for comparisons.
- **Language**: Turkish; English spoken widely in tourist areas.
- **Safety**: Generally safe; pickpocketing in bazaar areas; unofficial money-changers should be avoided.
- **Best time to visit**: April–May and September–October for mild weather (15–25°C) and fewer crowds.
- **Budget**: Excellent value; mid-range USD 60–110/day. Accommodation and food are much cheaper than Western Europe.
