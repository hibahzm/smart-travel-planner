# South Africa (Cape Town and Beyond)

## Overview
South Africa offers one of the world's most diverse travel experiences: Cape Town consistently ranks among the planet's most beautiful cities, the Garden Route unfolds 300 km of dramatic coastline, and the Kruger National Park provides world-class Big Five wildlife viewing. South Africa is the most developed African nation for tourism infrastructure, yet it retains genuine wilderness, a complex and compelling history, and a cultural richness — 11 official languages — that surprises almost every first-time visitor.

## Getting There
O.R. Tambo International Airport (JNB) in Johannesburg and Cape Town International Airport (CPT) are the main entry points. Johannesburg is better connected internationally; most intercontinental travellers fly JNB and connect to Cape Town (2h domestic) or take an overnight train. Flights from London take ~11 hours, from New York ~17 hours. Kenya Airways (Nairobi hub), Ethiopian Airlines (Addis Ababa), and Emirates (Dubai) are popular connecting routes.

## Cape Town
- **Table Mountain** — the defining landmark; the cable car ascent (or 2–3 hour hike) reveals a flat-topped mountain with fynbos (unique Cape flora) and 360° views over the Cape Peninsula. Book cable car in advance; closes in high winds.
- **V&A Waterfront** — working harbour; Zeitz MOCAA (Museum of Contemporary Art Africa; world's largest collection of contemporary African art); excellent restaurants; the Two Oceans Aquarium.
- **Robben Island** — where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned for 18 of his 27 years; boat trips run from the V&A; tours are led by former political prisoners.
- **Bo-Kaap** — brightly painted Cape Malay houses on the slopes of Signal Hill; a Muslim neighbourhood with one of Africa's best curry scenes.
- **Camps Bay** — glamorous beach suburb below the Twelve Apostles mountain range; the most beautiful urban beach in Africa.
- **Kirstenbosch Botanical Gardens** — on the eastern slopes of Table Mountain; one of the world's great botanical gardens; summer concerts on Sunday evenings.
- **Cape Point and the Cape Peninsula** — the dramatic southern tip; Cape Point National Park (entrance fee); the Cape of Good Hope; African penguins at Boulders Beach near Simon's Town.

## Garden Route
The 300 km coastal route from Mossel Bay to Storms River is one of Africa's great road trips:
- **Knysna** — lagoon town with the famous Knysna Heads (sandstone cliffs at the lagoon entrance); Knysna oysters are legendary.
- **Plettenberg Bay** — upmarket resort town; whale watching (July–November) and dolphin encounters.
- **Tsitsikamma National Park** — ancient forest, dramatic coastline; the Otter Trail (5-day hiking trail) is South Africa's most celebrated trek. The suspension bridge at Storms River Mouth is popular.
- **Oudtshoorn** — ostrich farm capital; the Cango Caves (underground stalactite formations) are nearby.
- **Wilderness** — 18 km of empty beach; superb birdwatching in the lagoon system.

## Kruger National Park and Safari
South Africa's largest game reserve (19,485 km²) borders Mozambique and Zimbabwe.
- **Big Five** — lion, elephant, buffalo, leopard, and rhino are all present; elephant and lion are commonly seen; leopard sightings require patience.
- **Self-drive safaris** — uniquely possible in Kruger; hire a car, follow the tar and gravel roads, and spot animals independently. Very different from guided safari.
- **Best game-viewing areas**: the South (Skukuza, Satara) for lion; the North (Phalaborwa, Punda Maria) for elephant herds; the Sabi Sands private reserve (adjacent to Kruger) for exceptional leopard sightings.
- **Seasons**: May–September (dry winter) is best for wildlife viewing as animals congregate at water sources and vegetation thins. October–April (wet summer) brings dramatic skies, migratory birds, and newborn animals.
- **Private game reserves** — Sabi Sands, Singita, and &Beyond Phinda offer luxury lodge safaris with guided game drives (USD 500–1,500/night all-inclusive).

## Wine Country — Stellenbosch and Franschhoek
Just 45 minutes from Cape Town:
- **Stellenbosch** — 200 wineries; oak-shaded streets with Cape Dutch architecture; the wine route covers Pinotage, Cabernet Sauvignon, and Chenin Blanc.
- **Franschhoek** — settled by Huguenot refugees in 1688; France-in-Africa vibe; the Franschhoek Motor Museum; exceptional restaurants including The Test Kitchen (one of Africa's best).

## Eat and Drink
- **Braai** — the South African barbecue; boerewors (farm sausage), sosaties (skewered marinated meat), and pap (maize porridge); a social institution.
- **Cape Malay cuisine** — Bo-Kaap's curries (bobotie, bredie, koeksisters); fragrant with cinnamon, cardamom, and apricot.
- **Braaibroodjie** — grilled cheese and tomato sandwich made over coals.
- **Biltong** — spiced dried game or beef; the omnipresent South African snack.
- **Wine** — Pinotage (a South African variety), Chenin Blanc, and Cabernet are excellent and affordable; wine farms offer tastings from ZAR 100–200.
- **Cape Town restaurants** — The Test Kitchen and La Colombe rank among Africa's best; Bree Street has the city's most vibrant dining strip.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: South African Rand (ZAR); 1 USD ≈ ZAR 18.
- **Language**: 11 official languages; English widely spoken.
- **Safety**: Crime is a significant concern; carjacking, mugging, and violent crime exist. Stay in reputable accommodation, avoid walking after dark in city centres, and secure valuables in hotel safes. Townships should only be visited on organised tours.
- **Best time to visit**: October–April for the Cape (warm and sunny); May–September for Kruger (best wildlife viewing). December–January in Cape Town is the peak of summer and peak of visitor numbers.
- **Budget**: Mid-range ZAR 1,200–2,500 (USD 65–135)/day. Cape Town is expensive by African standards; Kruger self-drive is excellent value.
- **Health**: Malaria prophylaxis required for Kruger and Lowveld regions; not needed for Cape Town or the Garden Route.
