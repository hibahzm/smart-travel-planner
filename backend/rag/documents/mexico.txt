# Mexico (Mexico City and Beyond)

## Overview
Mexico is one of the world's great travel destinations — a country of staggering cultural depth, extraordinary cuisine (a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage), dramatic pre-Columbian ruins, colonial cities, and a coastline spanning both the Pacific and the Gulf of Mexico. Mexico City is a 21-million-person megalopolis that ranks among the world's top food and cultural capitals; Oaxaca is a craft and culinary paradise; the Yucatán Peninsula offers Caribbean beaches and the greatest concentration of Mayan ruins in the world.

## Getting There
Mexico City International Airport (MEX) — now supplemented by the new Felipe Ángeles International Airport (NLU) — is the main hub for North America–Mexico connections. Cancún (CUN) is the dominant entry point for beach travellers. Guadalajara (GDL), Monterrey (MTY), and Oaxaca (OAX) receive some international flights. The US land border is the world's most crossed; bus services from Texas and California cities to Mexican border towns are well established.

## Mexico City (CDMX)
One of the world's largest and most underrated capital cities:
- **Centro Histórico** — the city's historic core, built on the ruins of Tenochtitlan (the Aztec capital); the Zócalo (the world's second-largest city square), the Cathedral, and the ruins of Templo Mayor (the main Aztec temple, excavated in 1978).
- **Chapultepec** — the world's largest urban park; the National Museum of Anthropology (the world's best pre-Columbian collection; the Aztec Sun Stone is here), Chapultepec Castle, and three major lakes.
- **Coyoacán** — Frida Kahlo's neighbourhood; the Frida Kahlo Museum ("Casa Azul" / Blue House) is unmissable; cobblestone streets and colonial charm.
- **Xochimilco** — ancient system of canals and "floating gardens" (chinampas); hire a trajinera (colourful barge) and join Mexican families celebrating weekends with music and food.
- **Roma and Condesa** — CDMX's most fashionable neighbourhoods; tree-lined streets, art deco buildings, world-class restaurants, and a thriving café culture.
- **Teotihuacán** — 50 km northeast; the pre-Aztec megalopolis; the Pyramid of the Sun (the third-largest pyramid in the world), Pyramid of the Moon, and the Avenue of the Dead. Go early (gates open at 8am) before crowds and heat.

## Oaxaca
The most compelling Mexican city beyond CDMX:
- **Monte Albán** — Zapotec hilltop city (500 BCE–700 CE); spectacular setting overlooking the valleys; UNESCO.
- **Santo Domingo Church** — arguably Mexico's most opulent Baroque church; the former convent houses the Museo de las Culturas de Oaxaca.
- **Mercado de Benito Juárez and 20 de Noviembre** — the covered markets selling mole pastes, local cheese (quesillo), chocolates, and memelitas.
- **Mezcal** — Oaxaca produces 85% of Mexico's mezcal; distilleries in the Tlacolula Valley offer tours and tastings; El Cortijo and Las Bugambilias are excellent.
- **Craft villages** — Teotitlán del Valle (hand-woven rugs), San Bartolo Coyotepec (black clay pottery), Arrazola (alebrijes painted woodcarving) are accessible day trips.
- **Guelaguetza** — the annual indigenous cultural festival (July); a spectacle of traditional dances, costumes, and music from all eight Oaxacan regions.

## Yucatán Peninsula
- **Chichén Itzá** — one of the New Seven Wonders; the El Castillo pyramid, Ball Court, and Cenote Sagrada. Go at sunrise (gates open at 8am, tour buses arrive at 10am). UNESCO.
- **Tulum** — the most photogenic Mayan site; a cliff-top walled city overlooking turquoise Caribbean water. The town of Tulum has evolved from hippie beach to luxury eco-resort destination.
- **Cenotes** — underground freshwater sinkholes; swimming and snorkelling in crystal-clear water; the Ruta de los Cenotes near Puerto Morelos and Dos Ojos near Tulum are the most accessible.
- **Izamal** — the "Yellow City"; a colonial town painted entirely in ochre yellow; the Convent of San Antonio de Padua is one of Mexico's largest.
- **Mérida** — the "White City"; capital of Yucatán state; extraordinary Mayan Museum, weekend artisan markets on the main plaza, and the best Yucatecan food outside of the villages.

## Beaches
- **Los Cabos (Baja California)** — dramatic desert-meets-sea scenery; the famous rock arch at Lands End; sport fishing, whale watching (January–March), and luxury resorts.
- **Puerto Vallarta** — lush jungle backdrop; Old Town cobblestones and beach clubs; whale watching in Banderas Bay (November–March).
- **Sayulita** — small surf town 40 km from Puerto Vallarta; consistent surf, low-key vibe, excellent tacos.
- **Huatulco (Oaxaca coast)** — nine bays of protected coast; largely undeveloped; snorkelling and diving.
- **Puerto Escondido** — the "Mexican Pipeline"; Zicatela Beach has one of the world's most powerful shore breaks.

## Eat and Drink
Mexican cuisine is among the world's great food traditions:
- **Tacos** — the foundation; al pastor (marinated pork from a vertical spit), carnitas (braised pork), barbacoa (slow-cooked lamb), and fish (Baja-style) are the essential varieties.
- **Mole** — Mexico's most complex sauce; mole negro (Oaxaca) with 30+ ingredients including chilli, chocolate, and banana is a lifetime's achievement to master.
- **Tamales** — masa dough with meat or cheese fillings wrapped in corn husks and steamed.
- **Mezcal and Tequila** — mezcal (artisanal, smoky, complex) from Oaxaca; tequila (from blue agave) from Jalisco.
- **Breakfast** — huevos rancheros (fried eggs with salsa and tortilla), chilaquiles (fried tortillas with salsa and cream), and tamales are the classic morning meals.
- **Street food** — Mexico City's tlayudas, memelas, and quesadillas cooked on a comal; Oaxaca's tlayuda (giant crispy tortilla); Yucatán's cochinita pibil (achiote-marinated slow-roasted pork).

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Mexican Peso (MXN); 1 USD ≈ MXN 17.
- **Language**: Spanish; English in tourist areas but much less so than other Latin American destinations.
- **Safety**: Highly variable by region. Mexico City (especially Roma, Condesa, Polanco, Coyoacán), Oaxaca, and the Yucatán Peninsula are generally safe for tourists. Some states (Guerrero, Sinaloa) have significant cartel activity; research current conditions.
- **Best time to visit**: November–May (dry season). June–October is summer/rainy season; hurricane risk on both coasts but often manageable.
- **Budget**: Excellent value; USD 50–90/day mid-range. Mexico City's restaurant scene punches at world-class quality for USD 20–40 for a full tasting menu with wine.
