# Rome, Italy

## Overview
Rome, the Eternal City, layers 2,800 years of history so densely that ancient ruins, Renaissance palaces, and Baroque fountains share the same street. The capital of Italy and of a global religion, Rome is simultaneously a living museum, a modern European city, and the seat of the Vatican. It rewards visitors who walk slowly, eat well, and get deliberately lost.

## Getting There
Rome is served by Fiumicino Airport (FCO, Leonardo da Vinci), 30 km southwest, and Ciampino Airport (CIA), used by budget carriers including Ryanair. From Fiumicino, the Leonardo Express train reaches Roma Termini in 32 minutes (€14); FL1 regional trains are slower but cheaper. From Ciampino, take the Terravision or SIT Bus Shuttle to Termini (€6–8). High-speed Frecciarossa trains connect Rome to Florence (1h30), Naples (1h10), and Milan (2h50).

## Getting Around
Rome's city centre is best explored on foot — most major sights are within a 3 km radius of the Colosseum. The Metro has only two lines (A and B) but covers key tourist stops: Termini, Barberini (Trevi Fountain), Spagna (Spanish Steps), Ottaviano (Vatican). Buses cover the rest of the city. Taxis are metered; fixed fares apply from airports. Avoid driving in the historic centre (ZTL zones carry automatic fines).

## Neighbourhoods
**Centro Storico** — Pantheon, Piazza Navona, Campo de' Fiori; the ancient and Baroque heart.
**Trastevere** — cobblestone lanes, ivy-draped facades, Rome's most atmospheric neighbourhood for evening dining.
**Prati** — quiet residential quarter west of the Tiber; closest to the Vatican; excellent local trattorias.
**Testaccio** — working-class neighbourhood built around a 19th-century slaughterhouse; Rome's most authentic food market and offal cuisine.
**Pigneto** — east Rome's artsy, gritty, student-filled neighbourhood; great bars and osterie.
**EUR** — Mussolini's utopian 1930s district with rationalist architecture and the Palazzo della Civiltà Italiana.

## See
- **Colosseum** — the world's largest Roman amphitheatre, dating from 70–80 CE; book combined tickets with Roman Forum and Palatine Hill in advance.
- **Roman Forum and Palatine Hill** — sprawling ruins of the civic and political heart of ancient Rome; included in Colosseum ticket.
- **Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel** — Michelangelo's ceiling is among the greatest artistic achievements in history; book entry weeks ahead in summer.
- **St Peter's Basilica** — the world's largest church; entry is free but dress modestly. Climb the dome for panoramic views (free with stairs, small fee for lift).
- **Pantheon** — perfectly preserved temple from 125 CE; the unreinforced concrete dome is still an engineering marvel. A small entry fee was introduced in 2023.
- **Trevi Fountain** — at its most atmospheric at dawn before the crowds arrive.
- **Borghese Gallery** — exceptional Bernini sculptures and Caravaggio paintings; strictly limited timed tickets; book 3+ weeks ahead.
- **Castel Sant'Angelo** — Hadrian's Mausoleum, later converted to a papal fortress; great views from the top.

## Do
- Throw a coin in the Trevi Fountain at sunrise for a quiet experience.
- Take an evening passeggiata through the centro storico.
- Visit the catacombs on the Appian Way (San Callisto or Domitilla).
- Attend a free outdoor opera or classical concert during summer festivals (Estate Romana).
- Explore the Protestant Cemetery in Testaccio, where Keats and Shelley are buried.
- Day trip to Ostia Antica — Rome's ancient port, far less visited than Pompeii and almost as impressive.

## Eat and Drink
Roman cuisine is honest, ingredient-driven, and deeply regional:
- **Cacio e pepe** — pasta with just pecorino cheese and black pepper; seemingly simple, notoriously difficult to perfect.
- **Carbonara** — egg, guanciale (cured pig cheek), pecorino; no cream.
- **Supplì** — fried rice balls with mozzarella; Roman street food.
- **Artichokes (carciofi)** — alla romana (braised with mint) or alla giudia (deep-fried Jewish-style) in spring.
- **Gelato** — avoid tourist traps with fluorescent-coloured mountains of ice cream; seek out artisanal gelaterie (Fatamorgana, Fior di Luna).
- **Aperitivo** — Campari Spritz or Negroni before dinner is non-negotiable.
- **Offal** — trippa alla romana, coda alla vaccinara (oxtail stew), and rigatoni con la pajata are Roman classics.

## Sleep
Luxury options include the Hotel de Russie (Rocco Forte), the Hassler at the top of the Spanish Steps, and J.K. Rome. Boutique mid-range hotels cluster in Trastevere and Prati. B&Bs and apartments in the centro storico offer good value. Avoid cheap hotels near Termini station unless you know the specific street.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR).
- **Language**: Italian; English widely spoken in tourist areas.
- **Safety**: Generally safe; beware of pickpockets near monuments and on Métro Line A.
- **Best time to visit**: April–May and October–November for mild weather (20–25°C). July–August is very hot and crowded but the city never fully empties.
- **Budget**: Mid-range €100–170/day. A full tourist day with major site entry (Colosseum + Vatican) will cost €50–80 in tickets alone.
- **Dress code**: Shoulders and knees must be covered to enter churches and the Vatican. Carry a scarf.
