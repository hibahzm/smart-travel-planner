# Rajasthan, India

## Overview
Rajasthan, the "Land of Kings," is India's largest state and its most romantic. An arid landscape of Thar Desert sands, ancient forts rising from rocky outcrops, and opulent maharaja palaces provides the stage for some of the subcontinent's most vivid cultural experiences. Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, and Jaisalmer each offer a distinct flavour of Rajput heritage, and the state is India's most compelling introduction for first-time visitors.

## Getting There
**Jaipur** (Jaipur International Airport, JAI) is the most common entry point; direct flights from Delhi (45 min), Mumbai (1h45), and seasonal international flights. **Udaipur** (Maharana Pratap Airport, UDR) serves Delhi and Mumbai. **Jodhpur** (Jodhpur Airport, JDH) has connections to Delhi and Mumbai. The golden triangle tourist circuit often connects Delhi–Agra–Jaipur by road or rail. The Rajdhani and Shatabdi express trains from Delhi reach Jaipur in 4.5–5 hours.

## Getting Around
**Taxis and auto-rickshaws** — easily arranged through hotels; negotiate rates for day trips or use Ola/Uber in larger cities.
**Trains** — Indian Railways connects the major Rajasthani cities; overnight sleeper trains between Jaipur, Jodhpur, and Jaisalmer are practical and comfortable in AC 2-tier class.
**Hired car with driver** — the most flexible way to cover Rajasthan; a car with a driver for 8–10 hours costs USD 40–70.
**The Palace on Wheels** — a luxury tourist train running a week-long circuit of Rajasthan's major cities; a bucket-list experience.

## Key Cities

### Jaipur — The Pink City
Built in 1727 by Maharaja Jai Singh II, Jaipur is the state capital and the most accessible introduction to Rajasthan.
- **Amber Fort (Amer)** — 11 km from Jaipur; a spectacular hilltop fort-palace with mirror palace (Sheesh Mahal), elephant rides (now controversial), and sweeping views.
- **Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds)** — Jaipur's most photographed façade; a 5-storey pink sandstone screen with 953 windows through which palace women observed street life.
- **City Palace** — the Maharaja's residence, still partly inhabited; excellent museum of royal artefacts.
- **Jantar Mantar** — UNESCO-listed 18th-century astronomical observatory; largest sundial in the world gives accurate time to 2 seconds.
- **Bazaars** — Johari Bazaar (jewellery), Bapu Bazaar (textiles), and Tripolia Bazaar (bangles); best shopping in Rajasthan.

### Jodhpur — The Blue City
The second city of Rajasthan; houses painted blue to indicate Brahmin households, now extending across the entire old city.
- **Mehrangarh Fort** — the most impressive fort in Rajasthan; 125-m high walls containing magnificent palace rooms and one of India's best museum collections. The views from the battlements over the blue city are extraordinary.
- **Jaswant Thada** — white marble royal cenotaph next to the fort; peaceful and beautiful.
- **Clock Tower and Sardar Market** — chaotic and atmospheric bazaar at the base of the fort.
- **Umaid Bhawan Palace** — part museum, part hotel (Taj property), part royal residence; art deco interiors and Rajput architecture.

### Udaipur — The City of Lakes
The most romantically beautiful city in Rajasthan, built around a series of artificial lakes.
- **City Palace** — the largest palace complex in Rajasthan; parts are now a luxury hotel (Fateh Prakash), museum, and public area.
- **Lake Pichola** — the central lake; boat rides to the Jag Mandir island palace and views of the Lake Palace hotel (now Taj Lake Palace, built on an island in the lake).
- **Jagdish Temple** — 17th-century Hindu temple a short walk from the City Palace.
- **Saheliyon-ki-Bari** — a garden built by Maharaja Sangram Singh for his queen's 48 handmaidens; fountains, lotus pools, and marble elephants.

### Jaisalmer — The Golden City
Remote desert outpost in the heart of the Thar Desert; a living medieval city still inhabited within its 12th-century sandstone fort walls.
- **Jaisalmer Fort** — one of the world's few living forts; thousands of people live inside its walls. Havelis (merchant mansions) with intricate carved facades surround the interior.
- **Patwon-ki-Haveli** — the most ornate of the havelis; five interconnected buildings; UNESCO-protected.
- **Camel safari** — 1 to 5 nights in the Thar Desert; Sam Sand Dunes (most commercialised) or more authentic outings near Khuri village; watching a desert sunrise from a dune is transcendent.

## Other Highlights
- **Pushkar** — sacred Hindu lake town with the world's only Brahma temple; camel fair in November.
- **Ranthambore National Park** — India's best tiger reserve; accessible from Sawai Madhopur; tiger sighting probability is among India's highest.
- **Bundi** — undiscovered gem with a step-well (baori), painted palaces, and almost no other tourists.
- **Chittorgarh** — the largest fort in India; site of three legendary jauhar (mass self-immolation) during Rajput resistance.

## Eat and Drink
Rajasthani cuisine is adapted to desert conditions — heavy on lentils, gram flour, and dried foods:
- **Dal Baati Churma** — the quintessential dish; baked wheat balls (baati) served with lentil dal and sweet churma; eaten at nearly every local meal.
- **Laal Maas** — fiery red mutton curry with mathania chillies; a Rajasthani signature.
- **Gatte ki sabzi** — gram flour dumplings in a spiced yoghurt sauce.
- **Ker Sangri** — desert beans and capers cooked with dried spices; found on most thali menus.
- **Lassi** — thick, sweet yoghurt drink; Jodhpur's Shahi Samand serves exceptional flavoured versions.
- **Rajasthani thali** — the best way to sample the cuisine; served in large restaurant-palaces in Jaipur, Jodhpur, and Udaipur.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Indian Rupee (INR); 1 USD ≈ INR 83.
- **Language**: Rajasthani dialects, Hindi; English widely spoken in tourist contexts.
- **Safety**: Generally safe; bargaining aggressively with drivers and touts is expected; watch for gem scams (offers to sell at a price and export abroad for profit).
- **Best time to visit**: October–March (cool, dry, 15–25°C). April–June is scorching (40–48°C). July–September brings monsoon rains.
- **Budget**: Excellent value; USD 40–70/day mid-range including a nice heritage hotel. Heritage hotels (former havelis and forts) offer extraordinary experiences from USD 50–150/night.
- **Religion**: Predominantly Hindu with significant Muslim communities; dress modestly at temples (remove shoes, cover heads in some places).
