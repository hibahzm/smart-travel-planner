# Buenos Aires, Argentina

## Overview
Buenos Aires is South America's most European city, a metropolis of 15 million built on Río de la Plata's wide estuary. "BA" offers world-class steak, the birthplace of tango, a frenetic food and nightlife scene, and a cultural energy that rivals any European capital at a fraction of the cost. Its crumbling Parisian boulevards, colourful La Boca neighbourhood, and the constant sound of bandoneons make it one of the world's most atmospheric cities.

## Getting There
Ezeiza International Airport (EZE), 35 km southwest, handles international long-haul flights. A taxi or rideshare (Cabify, Uber) costs ARS 5,000–8,000 (USD 5–8 at blue-dollar rates). The Tienda León bus service (ARS 3,500) runs to the city centre. Jorge Newbery Airport (AEP), 2 km from the city centre, handles domestic and regional South American flights.

**A note on currency**: Argentina has multiple exchange rates. The "official" rate is significantly below the informal "blue dollar" rate available at exchange houses (cuevas) and some hotels. Using the blue rate makes Argentina extraordinarily affordable for visitors.

## Getting Around
**Subte (Metro)** — 6 lines (A–H) covering the city centre and Palermo; cheap (ARS 70) and efficient during non-peak hours; crowded at rush hour.
**Colectivos (buses)** — over 170 routes covering every corner of the city; very cheap; cash is not accepted — only the SUBE card (available at kiosks).
**Taxi** — metered yellow-and-black taxis are plentiful, safe, and cheap; agree on meter use before departing.
**Cabify/Uber** — reliable and slightly cheaper than taxis; drivers are private cars.
**Walking and cycling** — the city's flat terrain and wide boulevards make cycling pleasant; Ecobici (free municipal bike-share) requires a local registration.

## Neighbourhoods
**San Telmo** — the oldest neighbourhood; colonial architecture, antique fairs (Sunday Feria de San Telmo), tango milongas in the streets and cafés, and the Mercado de San Telmo food hall.
**La Boca** — the brightly painted corrugated-iron houses of Caminito are the city's most photographed street; birthplace of tango; the Boca Juniors La Bombonera stadium is a pilgrimage site for football fans.
**Palermo** — the city's largest neighbourhood; subdivided into Palermo SoHo (boutique shopping, restaurants, galleries) and Palermo Hollywood (media agencies, trendy restaurants). The rose gardens of Bosques de Palermo are a green escape.
**Recoleta** — upscale European-feeling neighbourhood; the Recoleta Cemetery (home to Evita Perón's tomb and 4,691 elaborate mausoleums) is unmissable; the MALBA contemporary art museum is excellent.
**Puerto Madero** — reclaimed docklands; the most expensive real estate; the Faena Hotel is one of South America's most spectacular; accessible via the Puente de la Mujer (Santiago Calatrava rotating bridge).
**Belgrano** — residential, local, with a large Chinese community; Sunday market in Barrancas de Belgrano park.

## See
- **Recoleta Cemetery** — over 4,691 statues and mausoleums; Evita Perón's tomb (relatively modest compared to neighbours) is the most visited. Open daily; free.
- **MALBA (Museo de Arte Latinoamericano de Buenos Aires)** — exceptional Latin American modern art; Frida Kahlo, Diego Rivera, and outstanding Argentine artists.
- **Teatro Colón** — one of the world's great opera houses; acoustics and architecture rank among the world's best five; guided tours available; book performances far ahead.
- **La Bombonera** — Boca Juniors' stadium; the museum and stadium tour is excellent; attending a match is a life experience (book through official channels, not touts).
- **MNBA (Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes)** — free national fine arts museum with an excellent European collection.
- **Feria de San Telmo** — Sunday antique market filling the streets of San Telmo with tango performers, street food, and vintage finds.

## Tango
Tango was born in the working-class conventillos of La Boca and San Telmo in the late 19th century. Buenos Aires is its living home:
- **Milongas** — social tango dances held nightly across the city; El Beso, La Catedral, and Salon Canning are beloved institutions. No invitation needed; non-dancers can watch.
- **Tango shows** — Piazzolla Tango, Esquina Carlos Gardel, and Rojo Tango offer polished theatrical performances with dinner (expensive but spectacular).
- **Lessons** — group and private tango lessons available in San Telmo and Palermo from USD 15–50/hour.
- **Milonga outdoor** — free street tango performances in San Telmo on Sundays and in Puerto Madero on evenings.

## Eat and Drink
Argentine cuisine centres on beef from the Pampas — arguably the world's finest — and Italian-influenced pasta and pizza:
- **Asado** — the Argentine barbecue; not just food but a social ritual; chorizo, morcilla, costillas, bife de chorizo, and mollejas (sweetbreads) over quebracho wood coals. La Cabrera, Don Julio, El Pobre Luis.
- **Empanadas** — meat, cheese, or spinach-filled pastries; best in San Telmo.
- **Medialunas** — crescent-shaped croissants; Buenos Aires café culture revolves around medialunas with cortado coffee.
- **Alfajores** — sandwich cookies filled with dulce de leche; found in every kiosk.
- **Wine** — Argentina produces extraordinary Malbec (Mendoza) and Torrontés (Salta); ask for vino de la casa, it will be excellent and cheap.
- **Dulce de leche** — caramelised condensed milk; spread on everything from toast to ice cream.
- **Nightlife** — dinner begins at 10 pm; clubs fill after 2 am; the city runs until 7 am on weekends. Palermo is the nightlife centre.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Argentine Peso (ARS). Exchange at cuevas (informal exchange houses) for the blue-dollar rate rather than at banks; ask your hotel for advice. Prices effectively halve for foreign visitors.
- **Language**: Spanish (Rioplatense dialect with distinctive voseo and Italian-inflected intonation).
- **Safety**: Moderate; petty theft in tourist areas; avoid displaying expensive cameras or phones. San Telmo after dark can be edgy. Neighbourhoods like Recoleta, Palermo, and Belgrano are safe.
- **Best time to visit**: March–May (autumn) and September–November (spring) for mild weather (15–25°C). Summers (December–March) are hot and humid; winters (June–August) are mild.
- **Budget**: One of the world's best-value cities; mid-range USD 50–90/day including excellent accommodation, meals, and cultural activities. Fine dining costs a fraction of equivalent European restaurants.
