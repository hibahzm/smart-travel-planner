# Bangkok, Thailand

## Overview
Bangkok — officially Krung Thep Maha Nakhon — is Southeast Asia's most vibrant metropolis. A city of extremes where saffron-robed monks collect alms at dawn while club-goers end their night at the same hour, where gilded temples abut neon-lit street food alleys, and where world-class fine dining costs a fraction of what it would in Paris or New York. Bangkok is a sensory feast and an excellent base for travel across Thailand.

## Getting There
Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK), 25 km east of the city centre, handles most international flights. Don Mueang Airport (DMK), further north, serves budget carriers (AirAsia, Nok Air, Lion Air) operating domestic and regional routes. The Airport Rail Link Express from Suvarnabhumi reaches Phaya Thai station (city centre BTS connection) in 30 minutes (THB 150). Taxis from Suvarnabhumi cost THB 250–400 plus expressway tolls.

## Getting Around
**BTS Skytrain (BTS)** — elevated rail covering Sukhumvit, Silom, and Siam; air-conditioned and fast.
**MRT Blue Line** — underground line linking Chatuchak, Sukhumvit, and Lumphini to Chinatown and the new extension to Tha Phra.
**Chao Phraya Express Boat** — the most atmospheric way to reach Rattanakosin (old town temples); catch the Orange Flag service.
**Tuk-tuks** — negotiate the price beforehand; fun for short hops but slow in traffic.
**Grab** — Thailand's Uber equivalent; reliable and cheap (THB 80–200 for most city rides).
**Motorbike taxis** — fastest option in traffic; orange-vested drivers at every corner; agree fare first.

## See
- **Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha)** — the most sacred temple in Thailand, inside the Grand Palace complex; dress code enforced (shoulders and knees covered, no flip-flops).
- **Wat Pho** — famous for its 46-m reclining Buddha; also the birthplace of traditional Thai massage.
- **Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn)** — 82-m Khmer-style prang on the Thonburi side of the Chao Phraya; spectacular at sunset.
- **Grand Palace** — former royal residence and working religious site; allow 2–3 hours.
- **Jim Thompson House** — silk merchant's collection of traditional Thai houses filled with Asian art.
- **Chatuchak Weekend Market** — 15,000 stalls selling everything from vintage clothes to live exotic animals (the latter morally questionable); a world unto itself. Open Saturdays and Sundays only.
- **Chinatown (Yaowarat)** — Bangkok's oldest district; gold shops, dim sum, and one of the world's great street-food strips.

## Do
- Take a longtail boat tour through the Bangkok klongs (canals) to see riverside communities rarely seen by tourists.
- Get a traditional Thai massage at Wat Pho's massage school (THB 260/hour; book ahead).
- Visit the Damnoen Saduak or Amphawa floating markets on weekends.
- Catch a Muay Thai fight at Lumphini or Rajadamnern stadiums.
- Explore the Grand Palace area at night from a Chao Phraya River cruise.
- Take a day trip to Ayutthaya (80 km north), the ancient capital of Siam with spectacular ruined temples.

## Eat and Drink
Bangkok arguably has the world's best street food city status:
- **Pad Thai** — stir-fried rice noodles; Thip Samai near Wat Saket is legendary.
- **Tom Yum Goong** — hot-and-sour shrimp soup; a Thai national dish.
- **Som Tum** — green papaya salad; fiery and refreshing.
- **Khao Man Gai** — poached chicken rice; a simple dish perfected over generations.
- **Boat noodles** — thick, rich broth with pork or beef; best along the canal at Rattanakosin.
- **Rooftop bars** — Vertigo at Banyan Tree, Octave at the Marriott, and Sky Bar at Lebua (scene of The Hangover Part II) offer dazzling city views with cocktails.
- **Fine dining** — Gaggan Anand's restaurant consistently ranks among Asia's best; Le Du, Sorn, and Nahm offer creative modern Thai.

## Sleep
Bangkok has excellent accommodation at all price points. Luxury hotels — Capella Bangkok, Mandarin Oriental (the original, opened 1876), The Peninsula — compete for Asia's best hotel titles. Mid-range: the Sukhumvit and Silom corridors have hundreds of clean, comfortable hotels from THB 1,200–3,000/night. Budget: Khao San Road guesthouses from THB 300–600; better quality at hostels in the Ari or Ekkamai BTS areas.

## Practical Info
- **Currency**: Thai Baht (THB); 1 USD ≈ 35 THB.
- **Language**: Thai. English is widely spoken in tourist areas, hotels, and restaurants.
- **Safety**: Generally safe. Scams targeting tourists are common near the Grand Palace ("the temple is closed today" — it never is). Use Grab instead of street taxis to avoid overcharging.
- **Best time to visit**: November–February for cool, dry weather. March–May is brutally hot. June–October is monsoon season — heavy afternoon rains but rarely all-day downpours.
- **Budget**: Excellent value; budget travellers can live well on USD 30–50/day; mid-range USD 80–150/day; luxury is priced comparably to Western cities.
- **Dress code**: Modest dress required at temples. Topless sunbathing is not acceptable outside beach resorts.
